🎯 Ultimate Goal: A New Department — Independent Verification

This training is not just about certifying individuals. It is the first step toward building a permanent, independent verification function within government.

The ultimate goal: A dedicated department — separate from procurement, separate from vendors — with the authority and capability to verify whether systems actually deliver before they are accepted.

Can a government department be truly independent? Yes — independence comes from institutional design, not from the name on the door.

Existing examples of independent government verification functions:
• 🇺🇸 U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO) — reports directly to Congress
• 🇬🇧 UK National Audit Office (NAO) — reports directly to Parliament
• 🇨🇦 Office of the Auditor General of Canada (OAG) — independent of executive branch
These are government entities. Their independence comes from legal mandate, separate budget, direct reporting lines, and protected tenure — not from being "outside" government.

What makes a verification department truly independent?

  • Legal mandate — protected by law, not by administrative discretion
  • Separate budget — appropriated directly by Congress/Parliament, not through the agencies being verified
  • Direct reporting line — reports go to Congress/Parliament, not to the executive branch
  • Protected tenure — verification staff cannot be retaliated against for their findings
  • Transparent standards — GL formula, 8 parameters — open, replicable, no room for negotiation

No conflict of interest. No "player and referee" problem.
Just math. Just verification. Just accountability.

GL Verifiers are the nucleus of that department. This training plants the seeds. What grows from them — a truly independent verification function — is the ultimate goal.

🎯 Objetivo final: Un nuevo departamento — Verificación Independiente

Esta capacitación no es solo para certificar individuos. Es el primer paso para construir una función de verificación permanente e independiente dentro del gobierno.

El objetivo final: Un departamento dedicado — separado de las adquisiciones, separado de los proveedores — con la autoridad y la capacidad de verificar si los sistemas realmente funcionan antes de ser aceptados.

¿Puede un departamento gubernamental ser verdaderamente independiente? Sí — la independencia proviene del diseño institucional, no del nombre en la puerta.

Ejemplos existentes de funciones gubernamentales de verificación independiente:
• 🇺🇸 Government Accountability Office (GAO) de EE.UU. — reporta directamente al Congreso

  • Presupuesto separado — asignado directamente por el Congreso/Parlamento
  • Línea de reporte directa — informes van al Congreso/Parlamento
  • Mandato protegido — el personal no puede ser represaliado
