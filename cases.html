GL VERIFIER PROFESSIONAL HANDBOOK

Public Governance Outcome Verification & Administrative Friction Diagnostics
Part 1

Why This Profession Exists

1.1 The Structural Gap

Modern governments have invested trillions in digital transformation, AI-assisted service delivery, integrated eligibility systems, and procurement modernization. Yet despite massive investment, governments worldwide continue to face: public distrust, administrative friction, delivery failure, inaccessible systems, invisible procedural burden, and poor post-deployment accountability.

Governments still lack a formal profession responsible for independently verifying whether public systems actually improved real-world outcomes after implementation.

1.2 The Core Diagnostic Question

Most procurement systems ask: Was the system delivered? Was the contract fulfilled? Did the platform launch? But they rarely ask: Did citizens successfully navigate the system? Did administrative burden decrease? Did access improve? Did waiting times shrink? Did abandonment rates fall? Did real outcomes improve?

1.3 Policy Context: EO 14402 (April 30, 2026)

Executive Order 14402 mandates fixed-price contracts as the default, tied to clearly defined outcomes and performance-based metrics. It creates accountability at the contract formation stage. But it creates a new urgent question: Who independently verifies that performance-based outcomes were actually achieved? The GL Verifier is the answer.

1.4 The Problem of Pseudo-Digitalization

Many governments have not truly modernized service delivery. They have digitized outdated bureaucracy, automated obsolete procedures, and transferred paper friction into software friction. This is Pseudo-Digitalization: systems that appear modern while preserving inefficient administrative behavior underneath.

Part 2

Profession Definition & Independence

2.1 What a GL Verifier Is

A GL Verifier is an independent public systems professional trained to: diagnose administrative friction, verify delivery outcomes independently from vendors, evaluate citizen-facing performance from lived experience, identify structural workflow failures, assess post-deployment public impact, and issue independent outcome verification reports.

2.2 What a GL Verifier Is Not

  • Not a vendor consultant — cannot verify what they are paid to produce
  • Not an agency spokesperson — cannot independently report institutional failure
  • Not a procurement officer — cannot verify what they have accepted
  • Not a compliance auditor — compliance does not equal public outcome
  • Not a software engineer — technical success does not equal operational success

2.3 Why Independence Is the Core Requirement

The GL Verifier functions similarly to judges, inspectors general, independent auditors, and safety investigators. Independence is required because agencies may resist negative findings, vendors cannot independently verify their own work, political pressure distorts reporting, and financial interests create structural conflicts.

A system can be technically successful while being operationally dysfunctional. Only an independent professional can report this truthfully.
Part 3

Core Diagnostic Methodology

3.1 The GL Formula

GL = (Fs × Vn) / (Pd × Cf)

Fs (Flow Success Rate): completed outcomes / eligible participants (0–1)
Vn (Strategic Value): calibrated public value (1–10 scale)
Pd (Pain Duration): average active time burden in hours
Cf (Cognitive Friction): structural complexity multiplier (≥1.0)

3.2 Measuring Fs

Data sources: administrative records, abandonment rates, appeal outcomes, call center logs. If true eligible population unknown, calculate based on initiated applications and label as Fs(initiated).

3.3 Measuring Vn

1–3 = Marginal/local; 4–6 = Moderate, departmental; 7–8 = High, systemic; 9–10 = Critical, cross-cutting. Recommended panel: policy owner, subject expert, fiscal officer, citizen representative.

3.4 Measuring Pd

Counts only active time: document collection, form filling, mandatory appointments, corrections, proactive follow-up. Does NOT include passive waiting.

3.5 Measuring Cf

Score six dimensions, sum raw score, map to multiplier:

Raw score → Cf: 0–8→1.2; 9–16→1.5; 17–24→2.0; 25–32→2.6; 33–40→3.2; 41+→4.0+

3.6 Interpreting GL Scores

  • <0.5: Severe friction — burden exceeds value
  • 0.5–1.0: Fragile — many users abandon
  • 1.0–2.0: Functionally acceptable, improvable
  • 2.0–5.0: Efficient
  • >5.0: Very high flow — verify equity
Part 4

Field Operations Guide

4.1 Entering a System for Evaluation

Approach as a citizen would. Document every step including time elapsed. Record confusing instructions verbatim. Note every abandonment point. Identify handoffs. Observe AI-assisted steps. Collect evidence for Fs, Pd, Cf. Record Vn separately.

4.2 Friction Signal Detection

  • Repeated data entry → Cf
  • Confusing navigation → Cf
  • Delays → Pd
  • Manual coordination burden → Cf
  • Document gathering → Cf
  • Identity re-verification → Cf
  • Dead ends → Fs
Part 5

Procurement Verification (EO 14402 Alignment)

Pre-acceptance checklist: Outcome achievement, burden reduction, completion rate, abandonment analysis, AI authenticity, vendor claim verification, accessibility, error recovery.

Part 6

Standardized Diagnostic Report Template

Each report must include: Executive Summary, Citizen Objective, Workflow Reconstruction, Fs Findings, Vn Assessment, Pd Findings, Cf Findings, GL Score & Interpretation, Redesign Recommendations, Independent Conclusion.

Part 7

Certification Structure

  • Field Diagnostics (10 workflows) — 40%
  • Peer Review (3 critiques) — 15%
  • Final Diagnostic Project — 30%
  • Ethics Evaluation — 15%

Pass threshold: 75% overall. Ethics section mandatory pass. Renewal every 2 years.
