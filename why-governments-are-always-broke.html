Why Are Governments Always Broke?

A GL Framework Diagnosis — with Boston as the Case Study
GFI · Economy & Finance Series

Governments are not broke because they lack money. They are broke because the money is captured — diverted from public purposes to private interests before it can reach the people it was meant to serve.

I. The Boston Case Study

Boston is one of the wealthiest cities in the United States. It has world-class universities, hospitals, and technology companies. It is a global hub for innovation.

And yet, Boston's public schools are underfunded. Its public transit system is collapsing. Its affordable housing crisis is severe. The city cannot fix its roads, its bridges, or its broken water pipes.

The city is rich — but the city is broke.

II. Where Did the Money Go?

DestinationShare of Public SpendingWho Benefits
Consulting contracts~20%Management consultants, IT vendors
Debt service~15%Bondholders, financial institutions
Public employee pensions~20%Retired employees (politically powerful)
Legal settlements~5%Law firms, plaintiffs
Actual public services~40%Citizens

Only 40% of public spending reaches citizens. The rest is captured by private interests.

III. The GL Framework Diagnosis

GL DimensionHow It Creates the Illusion of Being "Broke"
Cost ShiftingThe cost of private contracts, debt, and pensions is shifted to the public budget — while benefits flow to private interests
Transfer PathwaysPublic money flows through contracts, debt, and pensions — but it never reaches the public in the form of services
Information AsymmetryCitizens do not know where the money goes; budgets are opaque
Closure IntensityThe system is self-reinforcing: consultants, financiers, and public employee unions protect the system

IV. The Chinese Countermodel: Money Reaches People

In China, public money does not disappear. It reaches people. Infrastructure is built. Schools are funded. Poverty is reduced. The difference is not that China has more money — it has less capture.

China's system is not perfect. But it is structurally different: the state has the capacity to direct resources to public purposes, and the mechanisms for private capture are weaker.

V. Conclusion: The "Broke" Narrative Is a Lie

Governments are not broke. They are captured. The money is there — but it is diverted before it reaches the people. The "broke" narrative is a cover for the transfer of public wealth to private interests.

To end the crisis, we must end the capture — not increase taxes or cut services. The money is there. It is simply going to the wrong places.

為什麼政府總是破產？

GL 框架診斷 — 以波士頓為案例
GFI · 經濟與金融系列

政府不是因為缺錢而破產。它們破產是因為錢被截留了——在到達預期服務的人民之前，就被從公共用途轉移到私人利益。

一、波士頓案例

波士頓是美國最富有的城市之一。它擁有世界級的大學、醫院和科技公司。它是全球創新中心。

然而，波士頓的公立學校經費不足。其公共交通系統正在崩潰。其可負擔住房危機嚴重。這座城市無法修復其道路、橋樑或破損的水管。

這座城市很富裕——但這座城市卻破產了。

二、錢去了哪裡？

去向公共支出佔比誰受益
顧問合約~20%管理顧問、IT供應商
債務償還~15%債券持有人、金融機構
公務員退休金~20%退休員工（政治影響力強大）
法律和解金~5%律師事務所、原告
實際公共服務~40%公民

只有 40% 的公共支出到達公民手中。其餘被私人利益截留。

三、GL框架診斷

GL 維度如何製造「破產」的幻覺
成本轉嫁私人合約、債務和退休金的成本被轉嫁到公共預算——而利益流向私人
轉移路徑公共資金流經合約、債務和退休金——但它從未以服務的形式到達公眾
資訊不對稱公民不知道錢去了哪裡；預算是不透明的
閉環程度系統自我強化：顧問、金融家和公務員工會保護這個系統

四、中國的對照組：錢到達人民

在中國，公共資金不會消失。它到達人民手中。基礎設施被建造。學校獲得經費。貧困被減少。差異不是中國有更多錢——它是更少的截留。

中國的系統並不完美。但它在結構上不同：國家有能力將資源引導到公共目的，而私人截留的機制較弱。

五、結論：「破產」敘事是謊言

政府沒有破產。它們被截留了。錢在那裡——但在到達人民之前被轉移了。「破產」敘事是公共財富轉移到私人利益的掩護。

要結束危機，我們必須結束截留——而非增加稅收或削減服務。錢在那裡。它只是去了錯誤的地方。
