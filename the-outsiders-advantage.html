The Outsider's Advantage

Why the truth about the fiscal crisis never appears in mainstream think tanks — because it exists only in the hands of those whose survival floor is secure and who need not compromise with the capitalist-landlord value system.

If you have listened to interviews with policy experts, or read reports from large think tanks, you may have noticed something odd: they sound highly similar. They speak of “fiscal discipline” and “structural reform”, and they almost never explain where the money went. Why do these highly educated experts keep repeating the same empty formulations? The answer is not that they are stupid. It is structural: they are bound by multiple mechanisms that steer them away from the underlying truth.

I. The Deadlock of Working for Income

Most people work because they have to. Rent is due; food must be eaten. If you stop working, you lose income; if you lose income, you lose the ability to survive. When keeping the job and feeding the household becomes the ultimate objective, the truth becomes a luxury — and even a hazard. The expert knows that transnational capital is avoiding tax. He knows, better still, that if he writes it down, next year's research grant is gone.

II. The Expert's Original Sin

Mainstream experts are not free people. They work for institutions funded by donors. If an expert finds a truth that threatens the donors' interests, he faces a choice: state it and lose the salary, or repeat the narrative and keep it. The funding mechanism is a sieve, not a verdict. It ensures the key question never appears in the output.

Beyond direct funding capture, implicit rules narrow their vision. Research cycles are tied to one-to-three-year funding horizons, making decades-long structural decay invisible. They depend on closed-door channels with financial circles; data on land valuation and tax burdens are held by industry lobbies and the Big Four. Mainstream macroeconomic models treat existing property rights as fixed constants, lacking parameters for scenarios where land increment belongs to the public.

III. The Shared Delusion: Ten Years of Global Fieldwork

Over the past decade, I have traveled the world advocating for Universal Basic Income (UBI). Through field observations across dozens of countries, I discovered a bizarre "shared delusion" dominating global policy discourse. Every nation's experts claim their fiscal crisis is unique, their demographics are unique, and their political failures are unique.

But the structural pathology is identical: transnational capital draining the tax base, and private oligarchs capturing public rent.

Another deeply ingrained delusion is the belief that every nation uniquely prioritizes "education". In reality, under neoliberal rules, education has been reduced to a narrow bottleneck of credentialism—a system that binds degrees to employment. It is no longer about cultivating critical thinking; it is a mechanism to extract tuition from families and delay youth unemployment. It is not a solution to the fiscal problem, but another form of cost transfer to the next generation.

IV. The Outsider Who Need Not Compromise on Values

This is why the GL framework comes from an outsider. I am a classically trained pianist working as a professional translator. I have never taken a cent from a company, a foundation, or a party.

I worked and lived in the United States for more than twenty years, living through the helplessness of being bound to housing finance. Only after inheriting this self-built rural house in China that my father left me did I come to know real housing security.

Land in China is not private property: urban land is state-owned, rural land is collectively owned. A rural homestead is allocated by the collective for a household to build on; the household holds a use right, not title — it cannot be freely sold, as a rule cannot be mortgaged for a loan, and what is inherited is the building, not the land. So it has no market price and appears on no list of things that can be pledged or traded; its value is computed only at expropriation.

Nor am I inside the distribution of that land increment: it arises at the disposal end, and it is not mine. I have no position that must be held, so my observation carries no directional pull from any particular interest.

Because I have no position that must be held, my observation carries no directional pull from any particular interest. Beyond this material exit, my capacity to question comes from the long linguistic training of translation—taking arguments apart sentence by sentence—and from a faith chosen via adult baptism, allowing me to dissect sacred terms like "progress" or "legitimacy" without acknowledging their sacredness in advance.

Much suffering is produced by human policy, not God's doing. Attributing it to Providence provides a sacred alibi for the status quo. Structural problems must be met with structural change.

While mainstream experts protect their jobs and fit within model boundaries, the outsider is free to rewrite the rules. The expert asks: how do we manage this system? The outsider asks: why does this system require our silence? The truth is not hidden; it has never been produced, because its production cost is higher than any existing institution is willing to pay. But it is now being written, on the open web, by those who need not compromise.

La ventaja del outsider

Por qué la verdad sobre la crisis fiscal nunca aparece en los think tanks dominantes: porque solo existe en manos de quienes tienen su piso de supervivencia firme y no necesitan comprometerse con el sistema de valores capitalista-latifundista.

Si has escuchado entrevistas con expertos en política pública, o has leído informes de grandes think tanks, quizá hayas notado algo extraño: suenan muy parecidos. Hablan de «disciplina fiscal» y «reforma estructural», y casi nunca explican a dónde fue el dinero. ¿Por qué estos expertos altamente formados repiten las mismas formulaciones vacías? La respuesta no es que sean tontos. Es estructural: están sujetos a múltiples mecanismos que los apartan de la verdad de fondo.

I. El callejón sin salida de trabajar por ingresos

La mayoría de la gente trabaja porque tiene que hacerlo. Cuando conservar el puesto y alimentar a la familia se convierte en el objetivo último, la verdad pasa a ser un lujo —y también un peligro. El experto sabe que el capital transnacional evade impuestos. Sabe, aún mejor, que si lo escribe, el año siguiente se queda sin financiación.

II. El pecado original del experto

Los expertos dominantes no son personas libres. Trabajan para instituciones financiadas por donantes. Si un experto encuentra una verdad que amenaza los intereses de los donantes, enfrenta una elección: decirla y perder el salario, o repetir la narrativa y conservarlo. El mecanismo de financiación es un tamiz, no un veredicto. Garantiza que la pregunta clave nunca aparezca en la producción.

Más allá de la captación directa, reglas implícitas estrechan su visión. Los ciclos de investigación están atados a fondos de uno a tres años, lo que hace invisible la decadencia estructural de décadas. Dependen de canales cerrados con círculos financieros; los datos sobre valoración del suelo y cargas fiscales están en manos de los lobbies inmobiliarios y las Cuatro Grandes. Los modelos macroeconómicos dominantes tratan los derechos de propiedad existentes como constantes fijas, sin parámetros para escenarios donde la plusvalía del suelo pertenece al público.

III. La ilusión compartida: Diez años de trabajo de campo global

Durante la última década, he viajado por el mundo abogando por la Renta Básica Universal (RBU). A través de observaciones de campo en docenas de países, descubrí una extraña «ilusión compartida» que domina el discurso político global. Pero la patología estructural es idéntica: el capital transnacional drenando la base impositiva y los oligopolios privados capturando la renta pública.

Otra ilusión profundamente arraigada es creer que cada país prioriza la educación de manera única. En realidad, bajo las reglas neoliberales, la educación ha sido reducida a un estrecho cuello de botella de credencialismo: un sistema que vincula los títulos al empleo. No se trata de cultivar el pensamiento crítico; es un mecanismo para extraer matrículas de las familias y retrasar el desempleo juvenil. No es una solución al problema fiscal, sino otra forma de transferencia de costos a la próxima generación.
