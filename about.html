01

About

Ping Xu is an international governance researcher and the creator of the GL Framework — a structural measurement system designed to evaluate whether public systems actually deliver outcomes to the people they are intended to serve.

Her work focuses on a persistent institutional blind spot: governments and organizations often measure spending, compliance, and implementation, while failing to measure the total structural burden required for a person to successfully receive a valid outcome.

The GL Framework was developed to make that burden visible.

02

The Structural Question

Why do well-intended systems still produce pain, delay, exclusion, and exhaustion?

The GL Framework began with a simple observation: when a system depends on resources it does not measure — family labor, unpaid caregiving, administrative navigation capacity, digital literacy, transportation access, or emotional endurance — those costs do not disappear. They are displaced.

The institution records a successful process. The public absorbs the hidden workload.

GL measures what conventional administrative metrics often do not: the denominator — the total structural work required to deliver one valid outcome to one person.

This same structural pattern appears across healthcare, unemployment insurance, education, housing, disability systems, and digital public services. The issue is not intention. The issue is delivery architecture.

03

Why the Framework Was Developed

Ping Xu grew up in Taiwan, where universal healthcare demonstrated how large-scale public systems can achieve broad coverage with relatively low administrative friction.

Later experiences observing long-term care and public service delivery systems revealed a different problem: a policy may exist formally, yet remain structurally unreachable to the people who need it most.

That gap between institutional intent and real-world delivery became the foundation of the GL Framework. The framework was developed not as a political ideology, but as a structural measurement methodology applicable across political systems, sectors, and jurisdictions.

04

Why This Matters in the AI Era

Speed is not delivery.

As governments and institutions deploy AI and automation at increasing scale, structural delivery failures can become faster, larger, and harder to detect.

Implementation is not proof.

The central question is no longer whether systems are digitized. The question is whether they actually improve human outcomes after implementation.

Technology can improve operational speed. It can also amplify invisible administrative burden. The GL Framework was developed to evaluate delivery through measurable structural analysis rather than implementation claims alone.

02

結構問題

為什麼立意良善的制度，仍然會製造痛苦、延遲、排除與耗竭？

GL Framework 起源於一個簡單的結構觀察：當一個系統依賴它沒有測量的資源——家庭勞動、無薪照護、行政導航能力、數位能力、交通可近性或情緒承受力——那些成本並不會消失，只是被轉嫁。

機構記錄的是流程完成。民眾承擔的是隱形工作。

GL 測量傳統行政指標常常沒有測量的部分：分母——把一個有效結果送達一個人所需要的總結構工作量。

同樣的結構模式出現在醫療、失業保險、教育、住房、身心障礙服務與數位公共服務之中。問題不在於意圖。問題在於遞送架構。

制度意圖與真實遞送之間的距離，成為 GL Framework 的基礎。這套框架不是政治意識形態，而是一套可跨政治體系、部門與管轄區使用的結構測量方法論。

Acerca de

Ping Xu es una investigadora internacional de gobernanza y la creadora del GL Framework, un sistema de medición estructural diseñado para evaluar si los sistemas públicos realmente entregan resultados a las personas a quienes deben servir.

Su trabajo se centra en un punto ciego institucional persistente: los gobiernos y las organizaciones suelen medir gasto, cumplimiento e implementación, pero no miden la carga estructural total que una persona debe atravesar para recibir un resultado válido.

El GL Framework fue desarrollado para hacer visible esa carga.

02

La pregunta estructural

¿Por qué los sistemas bien intencionados siguen produciendo dolor, demora, exclusión y agotamiento?

El GL Framework comenzó con una observación simple: cuando un sistema depende de recursos que no mide — trabajo familiar, cuidado no remunerado, capacidad de navegar trámites, alfabetización digital, acceso al transporte o resistencia emocional — esos costos no desaparecen. Se desplazan.

La institución registra un proceso exitoso. El público absorbe el trabajo oculto.

GL mide lo que las métricas administrativas convencionales suelen omitir: el denominador — el trabajo estructural total necesario para entregar un resultado válido a una persona.

El problema no es la intención. El problema es la arquitectura de entrega.

"Los sistemas diseñados para proteger a las personas pueden, sin intención, convertirse en sistemas que las agotan. La distancia entre la intención política y la entrega real es lo que mide el GL Framework."
