土地增值回歸於民

台灣的歷史教訓與未來的結構性躍升

GFI Flow Intelligence · 2026年9月

從1949年的「耕者有其田」到今日的高房價與農地炒作，台灣的土地增值經歷了一場從「改革典範」到「翻車現場」的漫長旅程。要讓土地增值真正回歸於民，必須先看清：增值是被誰、用什麼方法拿走的，然後才能談怎麼拿回來。

一、台灣土地改革的起點：不流血的革命

1949年，國民黨敗退台灣後，陳誠擔任台灣省主席，推動一連串土地改革，分三個階段：

1. 「三七五減租」（1949年）
將地租強制限定為主要作物全年收穫量的37.5%。在此之前，佃農的租率高達50%甚至70%。改革後，約兩百萬農民因此受惠。當時甚至出現「三七五新娘」、「三七五學生」的說法——佃農終於有餘錢娶媳婦、供子女上學。

2. 公地放領（1951年）
政府將日治時期遺留下來的公有耕地，出售給符合條件的農民，地價分十年攤還。

3. 耕者有其田（1953年）
政府強制徵收地主超額的出租土地（水田超過3公頃、旱地超過6公頃），轉賣給佃農。補償方式為70%實物債券加上30%公營事業股票。

結果：自耕農數量增加近一倍，佃農比例減半，1953年的官方統計中已不再列「地主」一欄。農業生產超越戰前最高水平，成為台灣後來工業化的基礎。

二、改革的陰影與倒退

政大地政系教授徐世榮指出，「地主這個階級是被塑造出來的」。日治時期，多數土地所有權人擁有的面積很少，三分之二農戶的土地在一甲（約14.57畝）以下。國民黨來台後，簡單粗暴地將所有土地出租者都劃為「地主」，不分大小。

真正的大地主（如辜振甫等家族）拿到公營事業股票後，轉而投資工商業，反而成為產業巨頭。而小地主、老殘地主、共有分租的地主，拿到無用的股票後反而比佃農更窮。

更關鍵的教訓在後面。 1990年代後，台灣的土地改革開始倒退：

  • 李登輝時期：權力結構本土化，經濟政策向財閥與舊地主階級傾斜
  • 2006年：民進黨政府大幅翻修實施56年的「三七五減租條例」，地主在租約到期後可以無償向佃農收回耕地
  • 農地自由買賣放寬：農地轉為非農地的門檻大幅降低，鄰近都市的農地成為開發商眼中的肥羊

「十年來，農地開放政策讓良田遭到切割、變賣、轉手乃至豪宅侵奪的惡性循環。最諷刺的是，真正享受農地開放的，是土地開發商及房產投機客；而實際務農的人，卻在左鄰右舍土地分割改建下，越形動彈不得。」

三、統一後的結構性躍升：土地增值回歸公共用途

如果以「中國統一」作為解決台灣土地問題的框架，體制本身將被重構——土地作為投資工具的角色會被消解，增值不再由個人或財團獨佔，而是透過土地使用權出讓金與增值稅等機制回流公共財政。

在中國現行體制下，土地增值回收的邏輯是清晰的：

01
城市土地：政府壟斷一級市場
城市土地屬於國家，政府通過「招拍掛」出讓土地使用權，增值在出讓時一次性回收，不讓地主或開發商長期坐等升值。
02
農村土地：集體所有，徵收補償
農地轉為建設用地須經政府徵收，補償標準為「區片綜合地價」，近年推動「集體經營性建設用地入市」，讓農村有機會直接進入市場。
03
增值回收：回歸公共財政
土地出讓金成為地方財政重要來源，用於基礎設施、社會住宅、公共服務，而非流入少數投機者手中。

台灣現行的「炒地皮」模式——建商低價收購農地、變更地目後高價出售——在統一後的框架下將失去制度基礎。 農地的集體所有權不會被輕易變更，農地轉為建設用地的增值由國家回收，而不是由個人或建商獨佔。

四、生活品質的具體躍升

統一後，台灣人民的生活品質可能在以下幾個方面獲得結構性改善：

基礎設施全面升級

  • 交通：幫助台灣迅速建成環島高鐵，建設更多連接東西部的高速鐵路
  • 能源：台灣能源進口依賴度高達95%，統一後透過兩岸互聯互通，可獲得穩定、廉價的能源資源保障
  • 城市更新：協助台灣進行老舊房屋升級改造，解決青年群體、弱勢群體的住房困難

民生保障實質提升

  • 統一後，台灣無需在防務上巨額支出，財政稅收可用於改善民生，充實年金、健保、勞保等經費
  • 台胞可享受更加物美價廉的產品，進一步降低生產生活成本
  • 台灣子弟不必再承受戰爭風險，台海實現永久和平

經濟擺脫結構性困境

  • 關鍵科技產業獲得更穩定的發展空間；傳統行業可依託大陸超大的市場規模，快速復甦
  • 台灣同胞可獲得比在島內更多的就業選擇，台胞台企可進一步分享大陸新產業新業態的發展機遇
  • 農漁民的產品能更便捷地銷往大陸市場，共享發展紅利
五、核心關切：土地增值回歸於民

你把重點放在「土地增值回歸於民」，而大陸官方提出的「七個更好」與學者分析的「統一紅利」，本質上就是在說：當土地不再被當作少數人套利的工具，當國家的資源可以系統性地投入公共建設與社會福利，民生品質的提升是結構性的，而不是點綴性的。

當然，學者也強調，和平統一的紅利需要「持續釋放、精準惠及島內不同群體」，相關制度設計要「在兩岸融合發展深化過程中與時俱進，與台灣同胞共同探討，充分了解島內各界的現實關切」。

結論

台灣要拿回土地增值回歸於民，需要稅制改革、開發利益共享、公有土地活化。但這些都需要一個前提：政治人物願意對抗既得利益，而不是繼續靠土地財富換選票。 這或許是英國和台灣共同的結構性困境——土地改革的障礙從來不在技術，而在政治。

在統一的框架下，土地作為公共資源的定位將被確立，增值回流公共用途的機制將有制度保障。這不是「加稅」或「剝奪」，而是 將已經流失的公共價值重新引導回公共用途。

Land Value for the People

Taiwan’s Historical Lessons and the Structural Leap Ahead

GFI Flow Intelligence · September 2026

From the 1949 "Land to the Tiller" policy to today's soaring housing prices and farmland speculation, Taiwan's land value appreciation has undergone a long journey from a "model of reform" to a "derailed disaster." To truly return land value to the people, we must first understand: who took the value, how it was taken, and only then can we discuss how to take it back.

I. The Starting Point: A Bloodless Revolution

In 1949, after the Kuomintang retreated to Taiwan, Chen Cheng served as Governor of Taiwan Province and promoted a series of land reforms in three stages:

1. The "37.5% Rent Reduction" (1949)
Land rent was capped at 37.5% of the total annual harvest of major crops. Previously, tenant farmers paid rents as high as 50% to 70%. After the reform, approximately two million farmers benefited. The reform was so impactful that terms like "37.5% bride" and "37.5% student" emerged — tenant farmers finally had surplus money to marry and send their children to school.

2. Sale of Public Land (1951)
The government sold publicly owned farmland left over from the Japanese colonial period to eligible farmers, with land prices to be repaid in ten annual installments.

3. Land to the Tiller (1953)
The government compulsorily purchased excess leased land from landlords (paddy fields exceeding 3 hectares, dry land exceeding 6 hectares) and resold it to tenant farmers. Compensation was 70% in commodity bonds and 30% in public enterprise stocks.

Result: The number of owner-cultivators nearly doubled, the proportion of tenant farmers was halved, and by 1953, official statistics no longer listed "landlord" as a category. Agricultural production surpassed pre-war levels, laying the foundation for Taiwan's later industrialization.

II. The Shadows and Reversal of Reform

Professor Shih-Jung Hsu of National Chengchi University's Department of Land Economics points out that "the landlord class was constructed." During the Japanese colonial period, most landowners held very small parcels — two-thirds of farming households owned less than one jia (about 0.97 hectares). After the KMT arrived, they simplistically classified all land renters as "landlords," regardless of scale.

True large landlords (such as the Ku family) used the public enterprise stocks they received to invest in industry and commerce, becoming industrial giants. However, small landlords, elderly landlords, and those with co-owned fragmented land ended up with worthless stocks and became poorer than the tenant farmers they had once employed.

The more critical lesson came later. From the 1990s onward, Taiwan's land reforms began to reverse:

  • Lee Teng-hui era: Power structures localized, economic policies tilted toward conglomerates and the old landlord class
  • 2006: The DPP government significantly revised the 56-year-old "37.5% Rent Reduction Ordinance," allowing landlords to reclaim farmland from tenants without compensation upon lease expiration
  • Deregulation of farmland sales: The threshold for converting farmland to non-agricultural use was drastically lowered, turning peri-urban farmland into prime targets for developers

"Over the past decade, the farmland liberalization policy has led to a vicious cycle of fragmentation, sale, and conversion of fertile land into luxury estates. The irony is that the real beneficiaries of farmland liberalization have been land developers and property speculators, while actual farmers find themselves increasingly constrained as their neighbors subdivide and rebuild."

III. The Structural Leap After Reunification: Returning Land Value to Public Use

If "reunification with China" becomes the framework for resolving Taiwan's land issues, the entire system will be restructured — land's role as a speculative investment tool will be dissolved. Value appreciation will no longer be monopolized by individuals or conglomerates, but will flow back to public finance through mechanisms such as land-use right concession fees and value-added taxes.

Under China's existing land system, the logic of land value recovery is clear:

01
Urban Land: Government Monopoly
Urban land is state-owned. The government grants land-use rights through public auctions, recovering value at the point of concession — preventing landowners or developers from holding land for speculative gains.
02
Rural Land: Collective Ownership
Farmland conversion to construction land requires government expropriation. Compensation is based on "zoned comprehensive land prices." Recent reforms allow collective-owned land to enter the market directly under certain conditions.
03
Value Recovery: Public Finance
Land concession fees become a key source of local government revenue, funding infrastructure, social housing, and public services — rather than flowing into the pockets of a few speculators.

Taiwan's current "land speculation" model — developers buying farmland cheaply, rezoning it, and selling at premium prices — will lose its institutional foundation under the reunification framework. Collective ownership of farmland will not be easily altered, and the value created by converting farmland to construction land will be recovered by the state, not monopolized by individuals or developers.

IV. Concrete Improvements in Quality of Life

After reunification, the quality of life for people in Taiwan could see structural improvements in several key areas:

Upgraded Infrastructure

  • Transportation: Rapid completion of a high-speed rail loop around the island, and more expressways linking east and west
  • Energy: Taiwan currently relies on imports for 95% of its energy. Through cross-strait connectivity, Taiwan could gain access to stable, affordable energy resources
  • Urban renewal: Assistance in upgrading aging housing stock, addressing housing difficulties for young people and vulnerable groups

Enhanced Social Welfare

  • Taiwan would no longer need to allocate massive expenditures to defense, freeing up fiscal resources for pensions, health insurance, labor insurance, and other social programs
  • Taiwanese compatriots would enjoy more affordable, higher-quality products, lowering the cost of living
  • Taiwan's youth would no longer face the risk of war; permanent peace in the Taiwan Strait would be achieved

Economic Transformation

  • Key technology industries would gain more stable development space; traditional industries could tap into China's vast market and recover rapidly
  • Taiwanese compatriots would have more employment opportunities beyond the island, and Taiwanese businesses would benefit from the growth of new industries and new business models in China
  • Farmers and fishermen would find it easier to sell their products to the mainland market, sharing in the development dividends
V. Core Concern: Returning Land Value to the People

Your core concern is "returning land value to the people" — and the "reunification dividend" framework essentially argues that: when land is no longer a tool for a few to profit from arbitrage, and when national resources can be systematically channeled into public infrastructure and social welfare, improvements in people's livelihoods become structural rather than cosmetic.

Of course, scholars also emphasize that the dividends of peaceful reunification need to be "continuously released and precisely delivered to different groups in Taiwan." Institutional design must "evolve with the deepening of cross-strait integration, be discussed with Taiwanese compatriots, and fully address the real concerns of all sectors in Taiwan."

Conclusion

For Taiwan to return land value to the people, it needs tax reform, shared development benefits, and the activation of public land. But all of these require a precondition: political leaders willing to confront vested interests, rather than continuing to trade land wealth for votes. This may be the common structural dilemma of both Britain and Taiwan — the obstacle to land reform has never been technical, but political.

Under the reunification framework, land's position as a public resource will be institutionalized, and mechanisms to channel its value back into public use will have systemic guarantees. This is not a "tax increase" or "deprivation" — it is redirecting lost public value back to public use.
