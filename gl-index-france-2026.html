A diagnostic report on governance friction, value capture, and cost shifting.

State-led value capture, but justice is compromised by market logic.

France scores 0.44 on the GL Index — a D grade. It is the Western country closest to a "land fiscal loop," using state-led infrastructure projects like the Grand Paris Express to recover publicly created value. But recent institutional shifts have allowed private developers to dominate public land development, weakening social housing supply and compromising justice.

Key Findings

  • Value capture shifted to market logic — The SGP mechanism for Grand Paris Express shifted from "tax-based" to "development-based" value capture, launching ~80 real estate projects and strengthening private developer dominance.
  • Social housing weakened — Development-oriented tools are structurally unfavorable to affordable housing, excluding vulnerable groups from station-area development.
  • Policy tool proliferation — France has over 180 housing policy tools, but while 500,000 units are needed annually, only 90,000 are built. Tool count does not equal governance outcomes.
  • Central-local friction — Centralization tradition conflicts with local autonomy, creating overlapping administrative procedures and repetition costs.
  • Trust and rule of law eroding — France's civic space was downgraded to "obstructed" in 2025; psychological and collapse costs are rising.

What 0.44 Means

France has state capacity and value capture mechanisms, but justice is eroding. It is neither the UK (aristocratic capture) nor the US (capital capture) — it is a hybrid of state intervention and market logic, with the two forces canceling each other out, resulting in unclear direction and fragmented execution.

Hallazgos Clave

  • Captura de valor orientada al mercado — El mecanismo SGP para el Grand Paris Express pasó de "basado en impuestos" a "basado en desarrollo", lanzando ~80 proyectos inmobiliarios.
