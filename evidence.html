The delivery gap is documented.
Not just observed.

Congressional inquiries, GAO findings, and federal audit data confirm what GL Framework measures: systems are implemented and funded, but outcomes for citizens are not independently verified.

U.S. Congressional Inquiries · Unemployment Insurance Systems
Senators Warren and Wyden on UI system failures
Multiple states received hundreds of millions in federal funding to modernize unemployment insurance systems. Congressional inquiry followed documented delivery failures.
U.S. Senate · 2021–2023
Congressional Inquiry
Senate Inquiry: UI Modernization Failures and Contractor Accountability
Senators Elizabeth Warren and Ron Wyden led inquiries into unemployment insurance modernization contracts, examining why states that received significant federal funding continued to fail claimants — with processing delays, inaccessible systems, and mass wrongful denials persisting after go-live.
Systems were modernized. Outcomes for claimants were not independently verified before or after go-live.
Massachusetts DUA · 2025–2026
State-Level Case
Massachusetts DUA: $46.6M Modernization · Delivery Not Confirmed
Massachusetts spent $46.6M modernizing its unemployment insurance system. The May 2025 upgrade refreshed the interface. Processing delays, a 70,000+ case backlog, and a 9× surge in appeals were documented in public sources through early 2026. No independent outcome verification was conducted.
Implementation confirmed. Outcome improvement not proven. GL = 0.059 (Flow Collapse).

U.S. Government Accountability Office · High Risk List
GAO High Risk Programs — Delivery failure as a documented pattern
The GAO High Risk List identifies federal programs with persistent management challenges. Many have been on the list for over a decade — funded, audited, and still failing to deliver outcomes consistently.
GAO · Updated 2025
GAO High Risk
Unemployment Insurance · On GAO High Risk List
The U.S. unemployment insurance system has been identified as a high-risk area by the GAO due to improper payments, fraud vulnerability, and inconsistent delivery across states. Federal investments in modernization have not eliminated delivery failure at the point of citizen contact.
Documented: funding exists, systems exist, delivery outcomes remain unverified at the program level.
GAO · Updated 2025
GAO High Risk
Medicaid · On GAO High Risk List
Medicaid has remained on the GAO High Risk List due to improper payments and program integrity challenges. The Iowa Medicaid case documents a $60M+ contractor-operated system where 72% of 247,522 disenrollments were procedural failures — the system lacked data, not eligibility evidence.
GL = 0.082 (Structural Failure). Denominator design, not fraud or underfunding, is the primary cause.
GAO · Updated 2025
GAO High Risk
Medicare Fee-for-Service · On GAO High Risk List
Medicare Fee-for-Service has been on the GAO High Risk List since 1990 — over 35 years. It remains there despite continuous investment and system upgrades, because improper payment rates and delivery inconsistencies persist as structural, not technological, problems.
35+ years on the GAO High Risk List. The problem is structural. Technology alone has not resolved it.
Medicaid · Improper Payments · Federal Audit Data
Improper payments as a delivery failure indicator
Improper payment data from federal audits documents a consistent pattern: systems process transactions, but the outcomes for eligible citizens diverge significantly from policy intent.
HHS / CMS · Annual Report
Federal Audit Data
Medicaid Improper Payment Rate
The Medicaid improper payment rate has been documented at approximately 15–22% in recent years, representing tens of billions of dollars annually. A significant portion of these improper payments are procedural — not fraud — caused by documentation failures, eligibility determination errors, and system design that imposes burden on claimants rather than resolving it internally.
Procedural errors, not fraud, are the dominant source of improper payments. This is a denominator problem.
Iowa Medicaid · OIG / CMS Review
State Case Study
Iowa Medicaid Eligibility System: 72% Procedural Disenrollments
Under Iowa's contractor-operated Medicaid eligibility system, 247,522 people were disenrolled. 72% of those disenrollments were procedural — the system lacked data to confirm eligibility, not evidence that people were ineligible. The ex parte renewal rate of 46% lagged the 60% national average, indicating systematic failure at the eligibility determination layer.
The system worked as designed. The design was the problem.
