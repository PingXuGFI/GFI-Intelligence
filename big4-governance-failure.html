The Big 4 and the Governance Failure

June 16, 2026 · GFI Flow Intelligence

The Big 4 — Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG — were originally audit and tax firms. Over the past twenty years, they have quietly taken over the core functions of government: policy design, IT system implementation, procurement reform, performance evaluation, welfare distribution, and digital transformation.

In many countries, governance has been outsourced to the Big 4.

This is not a conspiracy. It is a structural shift that happened without anyone noticing — and it has fundamentally changed how the state delivers services to its citizens.

“Governance has been outsourced — and no one verified whether it worked.”

The Five Structural Problems

The Big 4 are not evil. Their business model, however, is fundamentally misaligned with what governance requires.

1. They sell complexity — not solutions

The Big 4 bill by the hour, by headcount, by project size. The more complex the problem, the more they earn. There is a structural incentive to make simple problems complex, not to solve them.

A government asks: “Why is welfare delivery so slow?” A Big 4 answer often involves stakeholder mapping, PMOs, governance committees, maturity assessments, and transformation roadmaps. The core problem — no one verified whether the money actually reached the citizen — remains untouched.

2. They optimise for compliance, not outcomes

The Big 4 are excellent at compliance, controls, documentation, and risk management. These matter in auditing. But governance is not just compliance. Governments need to know whether policies actually land, whether people receive services, and whether costs translate into outcomes.

Too many systems today have: ✅ meetings ✅ reports ✅ dashboards ✅ KPIs — but ❌ citizens still don't get benefits, ❌ loans are still not processed, ❌ healthcare waiting lists continue to explode.

This is process success, delivery failure.

3. They fragment accountability

Big 4 governance design typically includes steering committees, program offices, vendor management offices, and cross-functional governance. Responsibility becomes so fragmented that no one is accountable for outcomes.

When failure occurs: Department A blames the vendor. The vendor blames procurement. Procurement blames the scope. The consultant says the recommendation was already delivered. No one is accountable for the failure.

4. They prefer PowerPoint over operational truth

A Big 4 deliverable is often a slide deck, an executive summary, a transformation roadmap. PowerPoint can describe the world — but it cannot verify the world.

A report might say: “Citizen service delivery improved by 32%.” When you ask: how was it calculated? Who verified it? Is it actual improvement — or just a case closure rate? — the answer becomes vague. Legitimacy cannot be built on narratives. It must be built on verifiable delivery.

5. They never challenge the procurement logic that feeds them

Governments buy: hours, people, deliverables, reports. They rarely buy: verifiable outcomes, measurable delivery, or outcome assurance. The Big 4 are the primary beneficiaries of this logic. If procurement shifted to “no outcome, no payment,” the traditional consulting model would collapse.

The Big 4 are not the cause. They are the institutional product of a broken procurement system.

GL = (Fs × Vn) / (Pd × Cf)
The Big 4 model increases Cf (cognitive friction), decreases Fs (flow success), fragments accountability, and makes verification impossible. The GL score moves toward zero.

A Global Pattern

This is not a theory. The evidence is everywhere:

  • UK NHS NPfIT: £12.7B spent, £10.1B net loss. Lorenzo deployed to 22 trusts out of 220. Contractors paid. Clinicians kept using paper records.
  • Australia Queensland Health: A$1.2B spent. 78,000 of 85,000 staff mispaid. IBM defined 24,000 pay combinations in two weeks.
  • Canada SAAQ: C$1.1B spent. Phases 2.5 and 3 cancelled. SAP had privileged pre-tender access.
  • UK Universal Credit: £22.7B unclaimed annually. Five-week structural wait. 110,000+ reassessment backlog.
  • UK ETA Border System: GL 0.04–0.09. Five vendors. Each fulfilled its contract. No one verified whether citizens boarded their flights.
“Experts complete their tasks. Citizens fail. This is the structure.”

Big 4 與西方治理失靈

2026年6月16日 · GFI Flow Intelligence

在許多國家，治理已經被外包給 Big 4。

這不是陰謀。這是一個沒人注意到的結構性轉變——它從根本上改變了國家向公民提供服務的方式。

「治理被外包了——而且沒有人驗證它是否有效。」

五個結構性問題

Big 4 不是邪惡的。但它們的商業模式，與治理所需的能力，從根本上是反向的。

1. 賣的是複雜度，不是解決方案

Big 4 按工時、按人數、按專案規模收費。問題越複雜，收費越高。把簡單問題複雜化，比把複雜問題簡單化更賺錢。

政府問：「為什麼福利發放這麼慢？」Big 4 的回答通常是：利害關係人分析、建 PMO、治理委員會、成熟度評估、轉型路線圖。但核心問題——沒人驗證錢到底有沒有到人手上——始終沒被觸及。

2. 優化流程合規，不是交付結果

Big 4 非常擅長合規、控制、文件化和風險管理。這些在審計中很重要。但治理不只是合規。政府需要知道政策有沒有落地、人有沒有拿到服務、成本有沒有轉成成果。

太多的系統現在是：✅ 有開會 ✅ 有報告 ✅ 有儀表板 ✅ 有 KPI —— 但 ❌ 民眾還是拿不到補助、❌ 學貸還是處理不了、❌ 醫療排隊還在爆炸。

這是 流程成功，交付失敗。

3. 把責任切碎，沒人對結果負責

Big 4 典型的治理設計包括：指導委員會、專案辦公室、供應商管理辦公室、跨職能治理。責任被切碎到沒有人對成果負責。

失敗發生時：部門 A 說是供應商的問題。供應商說是採購的問題。採購說是範圍的問題。顧問說建議已經交付了。沒有人對失敗負責。

4. 偏好 PowerPoint，不偏好營運真相

Big 4 的產出通常是簡報、高階摘要、轉型路線圖。PowerPoint 可以描述世界——但不能驗證世界。

報告可能寫：「公民服務改善了 32%。」當你追問：怎麼算的？誰驗證的？是真改善還是結案率上升？——答案開始模糊。正當性不能建立在敘事上。必須建立在可驗證的交付上。

5. 從不挑戰餵養自己的採購邏輯

政府採購買的是：人力、工時、產出、報告。很少買：可驗證成果、可衡量的交付、成果保證。Big 4 是這個邏輯的最大受益者。如果採購改成「沒成果就不付錢」，傳統顧問模式就會崩潰。

Big 4 不是問題的根源。它們是破碎採購制度的制度化產物。

Big 4 模式增加 Cf（認知摩擦），降低 Fs（流量成功率），切碎問責，讓驗證不可能。GL 分數往零移動。

「治理被外包了——而且没有人验证它是否有效。」

五个结构性问题

Big 4 不是邪恶的。但它们的商业模式，与治理所需的能力，从根本上是反向的。

1. 卖的是复杂度，不是解决方案

Big 4 按工时、按人数、按项目规模收费。问题越复杂，收费越高。把简单问题复杂化，比把复杂问题简单化更赚钱。

政府问：「为什么福利发放这么慢？」Big 4 的回答通常是：利益相关者分析、建 PMO、治理委员会、成熟度评估、转型路线图。但核心问题——没人验证钱到底有没有到人手上——始终没被触及。

2. 优化流程合规，不是交付结果

Big 4 非常擅长合规、控制、文件化和风险管理。这些在审计中很重要。但治理不只是合规。政府需要知道政策有没有落地、人有没有拿到服务、成本有没有转成成果。

太多的系统现在是：✅ 有开会 ✅ 有报告 ✅ 有仪表板 ✅ 有 KPI —— 但 ❌ 民众还是拿不到补助、❌ 学贷还是处理不了、❌ 医疗排队还在爆炸。

这是 流程成功，交付失败。

3. 把责任切碎，没人对结果负责

Big 4 典型的治理设计包括：指导委员会、项目办公室、供应商管理办公室、跨职能治理。责任被切碎到没有人对成果负责。

失败发生时：部门 A 说是供应商的问题。供应商说是采购的问题。采购说是范围的问题。顾问说建议已经交付了。没有人对失败负责。

4. 偏好 PowerPoint，不偏好运营真相

Big 4 的产出通常是简报、高阶摘要、转型路线图。PowerPoint 可以描述世界——但不能验证世界。

报告可能写：「公民服务改善了 32%。」当你追问：怎么算的？谁验证的？是真改善还是结案率上升？——答案开始模糊。正当性不能建立在叙事上。必须建立在可验证的交付上。

5. 从不挑战喂养自己的采购逻辑

政府采购买的是：人力、工时、产出、报告。很少买：可验证成果、可衡量的交付、成果保证。Big 4 是这个逻辑的最大受益者。如果采购改成「没成果就不付钱」，传统顾问模式就会崩溃。

Las Big 4 y el Fracaso de la Gobernanza

16 de junio de 2026 · GFI Flow Intelligence

Las Big 4 — Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG — eran originalmente firmas de auditoría y consultoría fiscal. En los últimos veinte años, han asumido silenciosamente las funciones centrales del gobierno: diseño de políticas, implementación de sistemas de TI, reforma de adquisiciones, evaluación del desempeño, distribución de bienestar y transformación digital.

En muchos países, la gobernanza ha sido subcontratada a las Big 4.

Esto no es una conspiración. Es un cambio estructural que ocurrió sin que nadie lo notara — y ha transformado fundamentalmente la forma en que el estado presta servicios a sus ciudadanos.

“La gobernanza ha sido subcontratada — y nadie verificó si funcionaba.”

Cinco Problemas Estructurales

Las Big 4 no son malvadas. Sin embargo, su modelo de negocio está fundamentalmente desalineado con lo que la gobernanza requiere.

1. Venden complejidad — no soluciones

Las Big 4 facturan por hora, por personal, por tamaño del proyecto. Cuanto más complejo es el problema, más ganan. Existe un incentivo estructural para hacer complejos los problemas simples, no para resolverlos.

2. Optimizan para el cumplimiento, no para los resultados

Las Big 4 son excelentes en cumplimiento, controles, documentación y gestión de riesgos. Pero la gobernanza no es solo cumplimiento. Los gobiernos necesitan saber si las políticas realmente se implementan, si las personas reciben servicios y si los costos se traducen en resultados.

3. Fragmentan la responsabilidad

El diseño típico de gobernanza de las Big 4 incluye comités directivos, oficinas de programas, oficinas de gestión de proveedores y gobernanza multifuncional. La responsabilidad se fragmenta tanto que nadie es responsable de los resultados.

4. Prefieren PowerPoint a la verdad operativa

El entregable típico de las Big 4 es una presentación, un resumen ejecutivo, una hoja de ruta de transformación. PowerPoint puede describir el mundo — pero no puede verificar el mundo.

5. Nunca desafían la lógica de adquisición que los alimenta

Los gobiernos compran: horas, personas, entregables, informes. Raramente compran: resultados verificables, entregas medibles o garantía de resultados. Las Big 4 son los principales beneficiarios de esta lógica.

GL = (Fs × Vn) / (Pd × Cf)
El modelo de las Big 4 aumenta Cf (fricción cognitiva), disminuye Fs (éxito de flujo), fragmenta la responsabilidad y hace imposible la verificación. El GL se mueve hacia cero.
