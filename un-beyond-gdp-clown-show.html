Beyond GDP, Beyond Reason

One Year, 14 Experts, 31 Indicators — and Nothing to Show
Ping Xu · GFI Flow Intelligence · June 2026

The UN spent an entire year, assembled a 14-member "High-Level Expert Group," and did what exactly?

They told the world that "GDP is not enough."

That took one year? That took 14 people?

"The welfare of a nation can scarcely be inferred from a measure of national income."

— Simon Kuznets, 1934 — 91 years ago

"GDP does not capture inequality, poverty, or environmental degradation."

— Amartya Sen, 2000 — 26 years ago

"GDP is a poor measure of well-being."

— Stiglitz-Sen-Fitoussi Commission, 2008 — 18 years ago

Now it's 2026. The UN spent a full year — and reinvented the wheel.

Then they held a grand global launch. The Secretary-General himself stood on stage and called it a "historic milestone."

😂

What Did They Actually Produce?

A dashboard with 31 indicators.

About half are existing SDG indicators — already there. Already done. Already agreed upon years ago.

Four dimensions: current well-being, equity and inclusion, sustainability and resilience, foundational principles.

Translation: they repackaged existing work, slapped a "UN" label on it, and called it their own.

But Wait — It Gets Worse

The expert group themselves admitted: implementation challenges — data gaps, limited statistical capacity, countries unable to track these indicators.

They built something most countries can't use. Then declared victory.

After the report was done, it wasn't ready to use. It had to:

  • Be handed over to Member States for discussion
  • Wait for Member States to decide whether to adopt it
  • Allow each country to decide whether to use it ("country-owned")

"The next step belongs to Member States. The intergovernmental process starts now."

— UN Secretary-General

Translation: "We're done. Now you continue fighting. We're not responsible."

Even Nature couldn't hold back: "Going beyond GDP should not leave the SDGs behind."

The experts couldn't even agree on a headline indicator — because 31 indicators are too complex to compete with GDP's "single number."

No headline indicator. No enforcement. No implementation plan.

But they held a press conference. So that counts, right?

🤡

Of the 66 organizations the US withdrew from, 31 were UN bodies. The White House statement said: "US taxpayers have spent billions on these organizations with minimal return."

Pero Espera — Empeora

El grupo de expertos admitió: desafíos de implementación — brechas de datos, capacidad estadística limitada, países incapaces de rastrear estos indicadores.
