資本主義會讓人變蠢嗎？
—— GL 框架八大參數結構診斷

摘要
資本主義制度在創造財富的同時，是否也在系統性地削弱人類的認知能力？本文以 GL（治理正當性）框架 的八大參數——等待成本、重複成本、崩潰成本、信息不對稱成本、心理成本、機會成本、轉移路徑、閉環程度——對資本主義進行結構性診斷。診斷結果顯示：資本主義並非讓人「變笨」，而是透過制度設計，將認知資源從「公共理性」轉移至「資本信號」，從而產生系統性的智力退化。
Abstract
Does capitalism systematically undermine human cognitive capacity while creating wealth? This article conducts a structural diagnosis of capitalism using the GL (Governance Legitimacy) Framework and its eight parameters — Waiting Cost, Repetition Cost, Collapse Cost, Information Asymmetry Cost, Psychological Cost, Opportunity Cost, Transfer Pathways, and Closure Degree. The diagnosis reveals that capitalism does not simply “make people stupid,” but rather, through institutional design, reallocates cognitive resources from “public rationality” to “capital signals,” resulting in systematic intellectual regression.

一、問題的提出

「資本主義會讓人變蠢嗎？」這不是一個修辭問題，而是一個可被檢驗的結構性命題。

過去半個世紀，全球平均智商（IQ）在已開發國家出現 「弗林效應」逆轉（Flynn Effect reversal），同時，注意力持續時間縮短、批判性思維能力下降、陰謀論與認知偏誤盛行。這些現象與資本主義的擴張同步發生，但極少被歸因於「制度設計」本身。

本文放棄文化或道德批判，改用 GL 框架的八大參數，對資本主義進行一次解剖學級別的結構診斷。

二、GL 框架八大參數結構診斷

以下表格逐項診斷資本主義如何透過制度設計，系統性地影響人類認知能力：

# 參數名稱 參數說明 資本主義診斷
1 等待成本 制度讓人民承受的不必要延誤 高度剝奪： 資本主義將「耐心」金融化——等待被轉化為利息、期權與違約風險。個人被迫將認知資源投入「最佳化等待策略」（如何時買房、何時換工作），而非長期思考。等待成本過高，導致大腦偏好短期獎勵，削弱前瞻性思維。
2 重複成本 因制度設計而重複投入的資源與勞動 認知消耗： 現代職場中，大量工作屬於「低認知附加值的重複任務」——填表、回覆郵件、應付審計、重複會議。這些重複勞動不產生新知識，卻佔據了可用於深度思考的認知頻寬。重複成本越高，深度學習與創造性思維的空間就越小。
3 崩潰成本 制度失靈時造成的系統性災難 創傷記憶： 2008 年金融海嘯、2020 年供應鏈崩潰、2022–2023 年銀行倒閉潮——每一次資本主義的系統性崩潰，都對經歷者造成「認知創傷」。人們開始相信「任何系統都不可靠」，從而放棄長期規劃，轉向防禦性、低風險、低思考強度的生存策略。
4 信息不對稱成本 知識與資訊被壟斷造成的權力失衡 核心病灶： 資本主義的利潤核心來自信息不對稱——賣方比買方知道更多、雇主比雇員知道更多、平台比用戶知道更多。這導致普通人的「認知校準」失敗：他們無法根據真實訊息做決策，只能依賴「信號」（品牌、價格、排名），而信號本身是資本操控的結果。資訊不對稱成本直接削弱了公共判斷力。
5 心理成本 制度對人民施加的恐懼、焦慮與壓力 認知耗竭： 資本主義社會中，生存焦慮（失業、房貸、醫療）與比較焦慮（社會地位、消費競爭）持續消耗心理能量。根據「認知頻寬」理論（Mullainathan & Shafir），貧窮與焦慮會大幅降低有效智商。心理成本長期居高不下，等於對全民進行慢性的認知減損。
6 機會成本 因制度限制而從未發生的創新與可能性 看不見的智力： 資本主義將創新定義為「可獲利的創新」，因此大量無利可圖但具有公共價值的思考（如基礎科學、長期生態規劃、非商品化的藝術）被邊緣化。機會成本不是「浪費了金錢」，而是「浪費了人類的認知潛力」。這種浪費是隱性的，因此極少被納入制度批判。
7 轉移路徑 價值從公共領域向私人領域轉移的機制 知識私有化： 資本主義透過智慧財產權、專利、付費牆、演算法黑箱，將「公共知識」轉化為「私有資產」。這導致一般人接觸到的知識是被篩選、收費、甚至扭曲過的。當知識本身的流通被設限，公共認知能力自然下降——不是因為人們不想學，而是因為「值得學的東西被藏起來了」。
8 閉環程度 制度自我強化、排除外部挑戰的能力 認知封鎖： 資本主義形成了一個強大的閉環——資本決定媒體內容，媒體塑造公眾認知，公眾認知決定消費行為，消費行為反饋給資本。這個閉環將「批判性思考」排除在外，因為批判性思考不產生交易。閉環程度越高，系統就越難以自我修正，導致集體智力被鎖定在資本偏好的軌道上。

三、綜合診斷：不是「變笨」，而是「認知信號被資本接管」

從上述八項診斷可以歸納出一個共同機制：

  • 資本主義不直接降低智商，但它改變了「認知回報的結構」。
  • 在資本主義制度下，「能產生資本信號的思考」得到獎勵（如投資策略、消費決策、職場競爭），「不能產生資本信號的思考」被懲罰或忽略（如哲學思辨、生態倫理、歷史反思）。
  • 長期下來，人們的大腦被訓練成「資本訊號接收器」，而非「真理探索器」。
  • 這種轉變不是生物性的，而是制度性的認知重塑——這就是 GL 框架所診斷的「治理正當性失能」在認知層面的表現。

🧠 結論
資本主義不會讓你「變笨」，但它會讓你「變得更擅長回應資本的信號，而非回應真理、正義或長期共善」。這不是個人的失敗，而是制度的設計結果。GL 框架的診斷價值就在於：把「個人愚蠢」重新歸因為「制度失能」，從而打開治理改革的可能空間。

I. The Question

“Does capitalism make people stupid?” This is not a rhetorical question, but a structural proposition that can be tested.

Over the past half-century, average IQ scores in developed countries have shown a reversal of the Flynn effect, while attention spans have shortened, critical thinking skills have declined, and conspiracy theories and cognitive biases have flourished. These trends have occurred in parallel with the expansion of capitalism, yet they are rarely attributed to “institutional design” itself.

This article abandons cultural or moral critiques, and instead applies the GL Framework's eight parameters to conduct an anatomical diagnosis of capitalism.

II. GL Eight-Parameter Structural Diagnosis

The following table diagnoses, parameter by parameter, how capitalism systematically affects human cognitive capacity through institutional design:

# Parameter Description Diagnosis of Capitalism
1 Waiting Cost Unnecessary delays imposed by institutions High deprivation: Capitalism financializes patience — waiting is converted into interest, options, and default risk. Individuals are forced to allocate cognitive resources to “optimizing waiting strategies” (e.g., when to buy a house, when to change jobs) rather than long-term thinking. High waiting costs bias the brain toward short-term rewards, weakening forward-looking cognition.
2 Repetition Cost Duplicated resources and labor due to institutional design Cognitive drain: Much of modern work consists of “low-cognitive-added-value repetitive tasks” — form-filling, email replies, audit compliance, repetitive meetings. These tasks produce no new knowledge but consume cognitive bandwidth needed for deep thinking. The higher the repetition cost, the less room exists for deep learning and creative thinking.
3 Collapse Cost Systemic catastrophe when institutions fail Traumatic memory: The 2008 financial crisis, the 2020 supply chain collapse, the 2022–2023 banking failures — each systemic collapse of capitalism inflicts “cognitive trauma” on those who experience it. People come to believe that “no system is reliable,” abandoning long-term planning for defensive, low-risk, low-thinking-intensity survival strategies.
4 Information Asymmetry Cost Power imbalance from monopolized knowledge and information Core lesion: The profit engine of capitalism lies in information asymmetry — sellers know more than buyers, employers know more than employees, platforms know more than users. This causes “cognitive calibration” failure: ordinary people cannot make decisions based on real information, so they rely on “signals” (brands, prices, rankings), which are themselves manipulated by capital. Information asymmetry cost directly undermines public judgment.
5 Psychological Cost Fear, anxiety, and stress imposed by institutions Cognitive exhaustion: In capitalist societies, survival anxiety (unemployment, mortgage, healthcare) and comparison anxiety (status competition, consumerism) continuously drain psychological energy. According to cognitive bandwidth theory (Mullainathan & Shafir), poverty and anxiety significantly lower effective IQ. Sustained high psychological costs amount to chronic cognitive impairment for the entire population.
6 Opportunity Cost Innovation and possibilities never realized due to institutional constraints Invisible intelligence: Capitalism defines innovation as “profitable innovation,” thereby marginalizing unprofitable but publicly valuable thinking (e.g., basic science, long-term ecological planning, non-commodified art). The opportunity cost is not “wasted money,” but “wasted human cognitive potential.” This waste is invisible, and therefore seldom included in institutional critique.
7 Transfer Pathways Mechanisms that shift value from public to private domains Knowledge privatization: Capitalism transforms “public knowledge” into “private assets” through intellectual property, patents, paywalls, and algorithmic black boxes. As a result, the knowledge accessible to ordinary people is filtered, charged for, or even distorted. When knowledge circulation itself is constrained, public cognitive capacity naturally declines — not because people do not want to learn, but because “what is worth learning is hidden.”
8 Closure Degree The system's ability to self-reinforce and exclude external challenges Cognitive lock-in: Capitalism forms a powerful closed loop — capital determines media content, media shapes public perception, public perception determines consumption, consumption feeds back to capital. This loop excludes “critical thinking” because critical thinking does not generate transactions. The higher the closure degree, the harder it is for the system to self-correct, locking collective intelligence onto a trajectory preferred by capital.

III. Comprehensive Diagnosis: Not “Becoming Stupid,” but “Cognitive Signals Captured by Capital”

From the eight diagnoses above, a common mechanism emerges:

  • Capitalism does not directly lower IQ, but it alters the “structure of cognitive returns.”
  • Under capitalism, thinking that generates capital signals is rewarded (e.g., investment strategies, consumption decisions, workplace competition), while thinking that does not generate capital signals is punished or ignored (e.g., philosophical reasoning, ecological ethics, historical reflection).
  • Over time, brains are trained to become “capital-signal receivers” rather than “truth-seeking instruments.”
  • This transformation is not biological, but institutional cognitive reshaping — which is precisely the manifestation of “governance legitimacy failure” at the cognitive level, as diagnosed by the GL Framework.

🧠 Conclusion
Capitalism does not “make you stupid,” but it does make you better at responding to capital signals than to truth, justice, or long-term common good. This is not a personal failure, but an institutional design outcome. The diagnostic value of the GL Framework lies in reclassifying “personal stupidity” as “institutional dysfunction,” thereby opening up possibilities for governance reform.
