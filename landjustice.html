A Structural Critique from Britain to Taiwan and Hong Kong

Ping Xu · GFI Flow Intelligence · September 2026

This series brings together seven essays on land reform — from Britain's "Seizing the Moment" and "The Ownership of Land Value" to Taiwan's "Land to the Tiller" and "housing crisis," and to Hong Kong's "2047 institutional rupture" and "cage home humanitarian crisis."

The central question running through all seven essays is: who owns the value of land? This is not a technical question — it is a political question. Who has the power to decide who owns the value of land?

Land & Human Dignity
Core Argument

"How a society treats its land is how it treats its people."

All seven essays point to the same conclusion: the obstacle to land reform has never been technical — it has always been political. Whether it's Britain's aristocratic landowners, Taiwan's vested interest groups, or Hong Kong's colonial legacy and extreme capitalism — the forces blocking the return of land value to the people are always the same: those who have grown rich from land and refuse to let its value serve the public interest.

Land justice means turning land from a commodity back into a resource, turning value appreciation from private capture into public return, and turning housing from class privilege into a basic human right.

土地與人的尊嚴
核心論述

「一個社會怎麼對待它的土地，就是怎麼對待它的人民。」

七篇文章共同指向一個結論：土地改革的障礙從來不在技術，而在政治。 無論是英國的貴族地主、台灣的既得利益集團，還是香港的殖民遺產與資本主義極端化——阻止土地增值回歸於民的力量，始終是同一群人：那些靠土地致富、卻不願讓土地價值服務於公共利益的人。

土地正義，就是讓土地從商品變回資源，讓增值從私人捕獲變為公共回流，讓居住從階級特權變為基本人權。

Capítulo 7
Tierra y Dignidad Humana
Argumento Central

"Cómo una sociedad trata su tierra es cómo trata a su pueblo."

Los siete ensayos apuntan a la misma conclusión: el obstáculo para la reforma agraria nunca ha sido técnico — siempre ha sido político. Ya sean los terratenientes aristocráticos de Gran Bretaña, los grupos de intereses creados de Taiwán, o el legado colonial y el capitalismo extremo de Hong Kong — las fuerzas que bloquean el retorno del valor de la tierra al pueblo son siempre las mismas: aquellos que se han enriquecido con la tierra y se niegan a que su valor sirva al interés público.

La justicia de la tierra significa convertir la tierra de mercancía en recurso, convertir la plusvalía de captura privada en retorno público, y convertir la vivienda de privilegio de clase en derecho humano básico.
