GL VERIFIER PROFESSIONAL HANDBOOK

Public Governance Outcome Verification & Administrative Friction Diagnostics

Ping Xu · GFI Flow Intelligence · 2026 Edition

A system is not successful because it appears modern. A system is successful only when human beings can reliably obtain real-world outcomes with reduced friction, preserved dignity, and visible accountability.

Part 1 — Why This Profession Exists

1.1 The Structural Gap

Modern governments have invested trillions in digital transformation, AI-assisted service delivery, integrated eligibility systems, and procurement modernization. Yet despite massive investment, governments worldwide continue to face: public distrust, administrative friction, delivery failure, inaccessible systems, invisible procedural burden, and poor post-deployment accountability.

Governments still lack a formal profession responsible for independently verifying whether public systems actually improved real-world outcomes after implementation.

1.2 The Core Diagnostic Question

Most procurement systems ask: Was the system delivered? Was the contract fulfilled? Did the platform launch? But they rarely ask: Did citizens successfully navigate the system? Did administrative burden decrease? Did waiting times shrink? Did real outcomes improve?

1.3 Policy Context: EO 14402 (April 30, 2026)

Executive Order 14402 mandates fixed-price contracts as the default, tied to clearly defined outcomes and performance-based metrics. It creates accountability at the contract formation stage. But it creates a new urgent question: Who independently verifies that performance-based outcomes were actually achieved? The GL Verifier is the answer.

1.4 The Problem of Pseudo-Digitalization

Many governments have digitized outdated bureaucracy, automated obsolete procedures, and transferred paper friction into software friction. This is Pseudo-Digitalization: systems that appear modern while preserving inefficient administrative behavior underneath.

Part 2 — Profession Definition & Independence

2.1 What a GL Verifier Is

A GL Verifier is an independent public systems professional trained to: diagnose administrative friction, verify delivery outcomes independently from vendors, evaluate citizen-facing performance from lived experience, identify structural workflow failures, assess post-deployment public impact, and issue independent outcome verification reports.

2.2 What a GL Verifier Is Not

  • Not a vendor consultant — cannot verify what they are paid to produce
  • Not an agency spokesperson — cannot independently report institutional failure
  • Not a procurement officer — cannot verify what they have accepted
  • Not a compliance auditor — compliance does not equal public outcome
  • Not a software engineer — technical success does not equal operational success

2.3 Why Independence Is the Core Requirement

The GL Verifier functions similarly to judges, inspectors general, independent auditors, and safety investigators. Independence is required because agencies may resist negative findings, vendors cannot independently verify their own work, political pressure distorts reporting, and financial interests create structural conflicts.

A system can be technically successful while being operationally dysfunctional. Only an independent professional can report this truthfully.

Part 3 — Core Diagnostic Methodology

3.1 The GL Formula

GL = (Fs × Vn) / (Pd × Cf)

Fs (Flow Success Rate) — completed outcomes / eligible participants (0–1)
Vn (Strategic Value) — calibrated public value (0.8–1.5 for welfare/administrative systems)
Pd (Pain Duration) — equivalent burden multiplier (1.2× to 3.0×)
Cf (Cognitive Friction) — structural complexity multiplier (1.2× to 4.0×+)

3.2 Core Principle: Diagnosis Is Not Calculation

GL diagnosis is not putting numbers into a formula. Diagnosis is the professional judgment of extracting Fs, Vn, Pd, and Cf from messy public evidence. Government reports do not say "Fs = 0.44." They say "first payment takes 4–6 weeks," "appeals increased 9x year-over-year," "only 44% processed within standard timeframe." Your job: translate these into Fs = 0.44.

3.3 Data Source Hierarchy

Golden rule: Cross-validate. Any single source can be wrong. Multiple sources pointing in the same direction is a signal.

3.4 Measuring Fs — Flow Success Rate

Definition: Proportion of eligible participants who successfully complete the intended outcome.

Important: Fs is not "applications processed." Governments say "we processed X million applications." Fs asks: of those eligible, how many actually received what they needed? People who gave up, were wrongly denied, or never applied are not in "processed" counts.

Signals from public data: Direct success rate → use directly; abandonment rate → Fs = 1 - abandonment rate; wrongful denial rate → Fs may be underestimated; appeal surge → signal Fs likely low; excessive wait times → signal Fs may be low.

Case example — Massachusetts DUA: Federal standard 87% within 14 days; Massachusetts only ~44% processed within standard timeframe → Fs = 0.44.

When no direct data: Use abandonment rate, appeal rate, wait time estimates, or mark "insufficient data."

3.5 Measuring Vn — Strategic Value

Definition: Calibrated public value of the policy outcome, independent of delivery efficiency.

Cross-national benchmark: Emergency healthcare 1.45–1.5; unemployment insurance 1.2–1.4; disability benefits 1.3–1.45; tax filing 1.0–1.2; information services 0.8–1.0.

Case example — UK Universal Credit: Serves 8.34M households, replaced six legacy benefits, safety net for unemployment/disability/low income → Vn = 1.3/1.5.

3.6 Measuring Pd — Pain Duration

Definition: Equivalent pain time — converting waiting, repetition, appeals, rework, and emotional exhaustion into a multiplier. Baseline: 1.0× = reasonable expectation.

3.7 Measuring Cf — Cognitive Friction Multiplier

Definition: For a normal person — without legal training, without accounting background, without anyone to help — how hard is it to understand what this system wants from you? High Cf means the system has outsourced complexity to citizens.

Six diagnostic dimensions (score 0–max points):

Pd RangeInterpretation
1.2–1.5×Some inconvenience, acceptable
1.5–2.0×Significant burden, some may abandon

3.0×+Extreme suffering, system collapse

Cf multiplier lookup table: 0–8→1.2; 9–16→1.5; 17–24→2.0; 25–32→2.6; 33–40→3.2; 41+→4.0+

Case example — UK Universal Credit: Documents 8, Categories 9, Handoffs 6, Approvals 5, Recertification 5, Ambiguity 5 → total 38 → Cf = 3.2× (use 3.0×).

3.8 The Interaction of Pd and Cf

The denominator is Pd × Cf — not Pd + Cf. High Cf + high Pd creates explosive effect: can't understand rules AND wait months → give up. Massachusetts DUA: Pd=3.0×, Cf=3.0× → 9.0 → GL=0.059. If Cf were 1.5×, GL would be 2× higher.

3.9 Sensitivity Analysis

Test key variables at ±20%. In Massachusetts DUA: Pd or Cf -20% increases GL by 25%. Pd + Cf both -20% increases GL by 56%. If an official questions your numbers, do not argue. Run sensitivity analysis: "Even if you adjust my Cf down to 2.5, GL is still structural failure."

3.10 Complete Diagnostic Workflow

  1. Collect public data — audit reports, statistics, hearings, journalism
  2. Determine Fs — direct data, or estimate from abandonment/appeals
  3. Determine Vn — score based on service importance, benchmark
  4. Determine Pd — wait time multiplier + additional burden
  5. Determine Cf — six dimensions → raw score → lookup multiplier
  6. Calculate GL — (Fs × Vn) / (Pd × Cf)
  7. Sensitivity analysis — test key variables at ±20%
  8. Write diagnostic report — cite sources, note assumptions, provide recommendations

3.11 One-Sentence Summary

Fs asks: How many actually got what they needed?
Vn asks: How important is this service?
Pd asks: How long did you wait?
Cf asks: Did you ever figure it out?

GL is not calculated. It is diagnosed from public data. The formula is simple. The judgment is hard. That is the value of a GL Verifier.

Part 4 — Field Operations Guide

4.1 Entering a System for Evaluation

Approach as a citizen would. Document every step. Record confusing instructions verbatim. Note every abandonment point. Identify handoffs. Observe AI-assisted steps. Collect evidence for Fs, Pd, Cf. Record Vn separately.

4.2 Friction Signal Detection

  • Repeated data entry → Cf
  • Confusing navigation → Cf
  • Delays → Pd
  • Manual coordination burden → Cf
  • Document gathering requirements → Cf
  • Identity re-verification → Cf
  • Dead ends → Fs

Part 5 — Procurement Verification (EO 14402 Alignment)

Pre-acceptance checklist: Outcome achievement, burden reduction, completion rate, abandonment analysis, AI authenticity, vendor claim verification, accessibility, error recovery.

Part 6 — Standardized Diagnostic Report Template

Each report must include: Executive Summary, Citizen Objective, Workflow Reconstruction, Fs Findings, Vn Assessment, Pd Findings, Cf Findings, GL Score & Interpretation, Redesign Recommendations, Independent Conclusion.

Part 7 — Certification Structure

  • Field Diagnostics (10 workflows) — 40%
  • Peer Review (3 critiques) — 15%
  • Final Diagnostic Project — 30%
  • Ethics Evaluation — 15%

Pass threshold: 75% overall. Ethics section mandatory pass. Renewal every 2 years.

Part 8 — Integrity Covenant & GL Verifier Oath

I certify that my observations, scoring, and conclusions are based on honest diagnostic judgment, observable evidence, and commitment to public outcome integrity. I will not manipulate findings for political, financial, institutional, or personal benefit. I understand that the responsibility of a GL Verifier is not to protect appearances, but to reveal reality.

Part 9 — Instructor Calibration Manual (Internal)

For certified instructors only. Maintains scoring consistency and diagnostic integrity. Calibration review questions: Is observation reproducible? Is friction observable? Is evidence sufficient? Is redesign logic coherent? Is citizen perspective preserved?

Final Principle: Digital transformation without workflow simplification is often only automated bureaucracy. The mission of a GL Verifier is to ensure that public systems become simpler, more humane, reduce unnecessary burden, improve real delivery outcomes, and restore institutional trust.
