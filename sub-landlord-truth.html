The Physical and Economic Realities of Land and Housing

An Evidence‑Based Examination of Depreciation and Ownership Structures
GFI · Land & Housing Series

The housing crisis is not a crisis of supply or demand. It is a crisis of ownership. Physical depreciation is real — but it is dwarfed by the financialization of land, which extracts value from the real economy and transfers it to the asset class.

I. The Physical Reality: Housing Depreciates

All physical structures depreciate. Buildings age, materials decay, and infrastructure requires maintenance. A house is not a permanent asset — it is a consumable good that requires constant investment to remain habitable.

The cost of maintaining housing is real. But the price of housing is not driven by maintenance costs. It is driven by land values — and land does not depreciate.

ComponentBehaviorImpact on Housing Cost
Physical structureDepreciates (requires maintenance)Moderate (maintenance cost)
Land valueAppreciates (speculation, scarcity)Extreme (drives price)
Location valueAppreciates (infrastructure, demand)Extreme (drives price)

II. The Economic Reality: Financialization of Land

Housing is not a consumption good. It has become a financial asset — bought and sold for profit, not for living. The financialization of land has transformed housing from a basic need into a vehicle for wealth extraction.

  • Rent extraction: Landowners charge rent for access to land — producing no value, only extracting it.
  • Capital gains: Land values rise due to public investment (transit, schools, parks) — but the gains are captured by landowners.
  • Mortgage debt: Homebuyers take on decades of debt, transferring future earnings to the financial sector.

III. The Two Economies

The land economy extracts value from the real economy. It does not create value — it captures it.

Wages have increased 5-6x since 1980. Housing prices have increased 10-15x. The gap is not productivity — it is land financialization.

VI. Conclusion: Reclaim the Land Value

The physical reality of housing is simple: structures depreciate, land does not. The economic reality is perverse: land value is captured privately, while the cost of housing is borne by the public. The solution is not to build more housing — it is to reclaim land value for the public.

If public investment creates land value, that value should return to the public. Land value capture is not a radical idea — it is simple justice.

土地與住房的物理與經濟現實

折舊與所有權結構的實證檢視
GFI · 土地與住房系列

住房危機不是供需危機。它是所有權危機。物理折舊是真實的——但它被土地的金融化所壓倒，後者從實體經濟中提取價值並將其轉移給資產階級。

一、物理現實：住房折舊

所有物理結構都會折舊。建築老化、材料腐朽、基礎設施需要維護。住房不是永久資產——它是需要持續投資才能維持可居住性的消耗品。

維護住房的成本是真實的。但住房的價格並非由維護成本驅動。它由土地價值驅動——而土地不會折舊。

組成部分行為對住房成本的影響
物理結構折舊（需要維護）中等（維護成本）
土地價值增值（投機、稀缺性）極端（驅動價格）
區位價值增值（基礎設施、需求）極端（驅動價格）

二、經濟現實：土地的金融化

住房不是消費品。它已成為金融資產——為利潤而買賣，而非為居住。土地的金融化已將住房從基本需求轉變為財富提取的工具。

  • 租金提取：地主對土地使用收取租金——不生產價值，只提取價值。
  • 資本利得：土地價值因公共投資（交通、學校、公園）而上升——但利得被地主捕獲。
  • 抵押貸款債務：購房者背負數十年債務，將未來收入轉移給金融部門。

三、兩種經濟

經濟生產什麼誰受益誰付出
實體經濟商品、服務、基礎設施勞工、消費者、公眾納稅人、消費者

土地經濟從實體經濟中提取價值。它不創造價值——它捕獲價值。

六、結論：回收土地價值

住房的物理現實很簡單：結構折舊，土地不折舊。經濟現實是扭曲的：土地價值被私人捕獲，而住房成本由公眾承擔。解決方案不是建造更多住房——而是為公眾回收土地價值。

如果公共投資創造土地價值，那價值應該回歸公眾。土地價值捕獲不是激進的想法——它是簡單的正義。
