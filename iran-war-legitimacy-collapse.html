伊朗戰爭與美國霸權的合法性崩潰 —— GL 框架八大參數結構診斷

摘要
2026 年伊朗戰爭是美國霸權的「合法性壓力測試」。本文以 GL（治理正當性）框架 的八大參數——等待成本、重複成本、崩潰成本、信息不對稱成本、心理成本、機會成本、轉移路徑、閉環程度——對美國在戰爭中的戰略困境進行結構性診斷。診斷結果顯示：美國的治理失能並非戰術失誤，而是制度性的「合法性空心化」——其在戰爭消耗、盟友離心、資源瓶頸與金融動盪中，暴露了霸權治理的所有結構性裂縫。
Abstract
The 2026 Iran war serves as a “legitimacy stress test” for U.S. hegemony. This article applies the GL (Governance Legitimacy) Framework and its eight parameters — Waiting Cost, Repetition Cost, Collapse Cost, Information Asymmetry Cost, Psychological Cost, Opportunity Cost, Transfer Pathways, and Closure Degree — to conduct a structural diagnosis of the U.S. strategic predicament in the war. The diagnosis reveals that U.S. governance failure is not a tactical error but an institutional “legitimacy hollowing-out” — exposing all structural fractures of hegemonic governance through war attrition, allied defection, resource bottlenecks, and financial turbulence.

一、問題的提出：一場戰爭，一次合法性崩潰

2026 年初爆發的伊朗戰爭，原本被美國軍方預估為「四至六週」的速決戰。然而截至 2026 年 8 月，戰爭已持續近七個月，耗資近 400 億美元，國內汽油價格上漲 40%，總統支持率跌至 33%。

這不是一場「打不贏」的戰爭，而是一場 「治理合法性全面失靈」 的戰爭。本文不討論戰術細節，而是用 GL 框架的八大參數，診斷美國霸權為何在這一戰中暴露了所有結構性病灶。

二、GL 框架八大參數結構診斷

# 參數名稱 參數說明 伊朗戰爭中的美國診斷
1 等待成本 制度讓人民承受的不必要延誤 戰略癱瘓： 美國的決策體系在戰爭中暴露了致命的「等待成本」——國會與行政部門的制衡機制，在戰爭時期轉化為延誤與扯皮。從追加軍費到調整戰略，每個決策都因制度性延宕而錯失時機。這種等待成本不僅消耗財政，更消耗了公眾對「治理效率」的最後信任。
2 重複成本 因制度設計而重複投入的資源與勞動 無效轟炸： 美軍在戰爭中反覆執行「精確打擊」——同一目標被多次轟炸，因為情報系統與決策鏈條之間的「重複成本」過高。每次打擊都需要重新確認、重新授權、重新執行，大量資源被消耗在制度性重複勞動中，而非實際戰略效果上。
3 崩潰成本 制度失靈時造成的系統性災難 系統失能： 當美軍發現防空導彈庫存降至「令人擔憂的水平」時，整個國防工業體系無法在短期內補給——因為數十年來的美國軍工體系已成為「精準但脆弱」的系統。崩潰成本在這裡表現為：一旦供應鏈斷裂或庫存見底，整個作戰體系即陷入癱瘓，沒有備份、沒有替代、沒有冗餘。
4 信息不對稱成本 知識與資訊被壟斷造成的權力失衡 認知泡沫： 美國政府與軍方長期向公眾提供「樂觀戰報」，而前線真實狀況被層層過濾。這種信息不對稱導致了兩個後果：公眾無法根據真實情況做出政治判斷（如是否支持續戰），而決策者自身也因「信息繭房」而誤判戰局。資訊不對稱成本直接導致了戰略誤判的累積。
5 心理成本 制度對人民施加的恐懼、焦慮與壓力 戰爭疲勞： 戰爭推高油價與通脹，美國中產家庭每月額外支出增加數百美元。同時，戰爭新聞持續製造焦慮——「是否會擴大成區域戰爭？」「是否會被徵召？」。心理成本不僅來自經濟壓力，更來自制度對「不確定性」的製造與放任。這種長期焦慮，正在侵蝕美國公民的認知與判斷力。
6 機會成本 因制度限制而從未發生的創新與可能性 戰略單一： 美國在伊朗問題上只有「軍事打擊」與「經濟制裁」兩種工具，其他可能性——如區域安全合作、能源協議、外交突破——因為制度與意識形態的限制而從未被認真考慮。機會成本在此表現為：美國從未嘗試建構一個不依賴戰爭的中東秩序，而這種「想像力的制度性喪失」正是其霸權衰退的根源之一。
7 轉移路徑 價值從公共領域向私人領域轉移的機制 戰爭私有化： 美國戰爭機器的核心是「軍事工業複合體」——公共財政的戰爭支出，透過國防合約轉化為私人企業的利潤。波音、雷神、洛克希德·馬丁等公司在戰爭中獲得了巨額訂單，而公共債務卻持續累積。轉移路徑在這裡清晰可見：戰爭成本由全民承擔，戰爭利潤由少數資本家享有——這正是治理正當性被掏空的核心機制。
8 閉環程度 制度自我強化、排除外部挑戰的能力 資訊繭房： 美國的媒體—智庫—國會—軍工體系形成了一個嚴密的閉環。批評戰爭的聲音被標籤為「不愛國」，而支持戰爭的論述則被無限放大。這個閉環不僅排除了外部挑戰（如國際社會的反戰聲浪），也排除了內部反思（如對戰爭正當性的質疑）。閉環程度越高，美國就越難以從錯誤戰略中抽身——因為認錯的成本遠高於繼續錯下去的成本。

三、綜合診斷：霸權的合法性崩潰

從上述八項診斷可以歸納出一個完整的因果鏈：

  • 信息不對稱成本 讓美國決策者與公眾都活在「認知泡沫」中；
  • 等待成本 與 重複成本 讓戰爭效率持續降低；
  • 崩潰成本 暴露了後勤與供應鏈的系統性脆弱；
  • 心理成本 與 機會成本 讓美國逐漸喪失戰略想像力；
  • 轉移路徑 將戰爭利潤私有化，進一步腐蝕公共信任；
  • 閉環程度 則讓整個系統無法自我修正，陷入「越錯越堅持」的惡性循環。

這就是 GL 框架所定義的 「治理正當性失能」——不是某個政策失誤，而是整個治理結構的系統性崩潰。

⚔️ 結論
伊朗戰爭不是美國霸權的「外部衝擊」，而是其內部合法性危機的「外部化呈現」。美國的困境不在於打不贏伊朗，而在於 它已經失去了「為何而戰」的治理正當性——無論是對國內民眾、對盟友，還是對國際社會。當一個霸權的「治理輸出」只剩戰爭與混亂時，它的崩潰就不再是「會不會」，而是「什麼時候」。

I. The Question: One War, One Legitimacy Collapse

The Iran war, which broke out in early 2026, was initially estimated by the U.S. military as a “four-to-six-week” rapid campaign. But as of August 2026, the war has lasted nearly seven months, costing nearly $40 billion, driving U.S. gasoline prices up 40%, and dropping presidential approval to 33%.

This is not a war that “cannot be won,” but a war of “total governance legitimacy failure.” This article does not discuss tactics, but uses the GL Framework's eight parameters to diagnose why U.S. hegemony exposed all its structural pathologies in this war.

II. GL Eight-Parameter Structural Diagnosis

# Parameter Description U.S. Diagnosis in the Iran War
1 Waiting Cost Unnecessary delays imposed by institutions Strategic paralysis: The U.S. decision-making system revealed a fatal “waiting cost” during the war. The checks-and-balances between Congress and the executive branch translated into delays and bickering in wartime. From supplemental funding to strategic adjustments, every decision missed opportunities due to institutional delays. This waiting cost consumes not only fiscal resources but also public trust in “governance efficiency.”
2 Repetition Cost Duplicated resources and labor due to institutional design Ineffective bombardment: The U.S. military repeatedly executed “precision strikes” — hitting the same targets multiple times because of the high “repetition cost” between intelligence systems and decision chains. Each strike required re-verification, re-authorization, and re-execution, consuming vast resources in institutional duplication rather than actual strategic effect.
3 Collapse Cost Systemic catastrophe when institutions fail Systemic dysfunction: When the U.S. military discovered that air-defense missile inventories had fallen to “concerning levels,” the entire defense industrial system could not replenish them in the short term — because the U.S. military-industrial complex has become a “precise but fragile” system. Collapse cost here manifests as: once the supply chain breaks or inventories run out, the entire combat system collapses — with no backups, no alternatives, no redundancy.
4 Information Asymmetry Cost Power imbalance from monopolized knowledge and information Cognitive bubble: The U.S. government and military have long provided “optimistic war reports” to the public, while frontline realities are filtered through multiple layers. This information asymmetry produced two consequences: the public cannot make political judgments based on truth (e.g., whether to continue the war), and decision-makers themselves misjudge the situation due to their own “information cocoons.” Information asymmetry cost directly leads to cumulative strategic miscalculation.
5 Psychological Cost Fear, anxiety, and stress imposed by institutions War fatigue: The war drives up oil prices and inflation, adding hundreds of dollars in monthly expenses for U.S. middle-class families. Meanwhile, war news continuously generates anxiety — “Will it expand into a regional war?” “Will I be drafted?” Psychological cost comes not only from economic pressure but also from the institutional creation and neglect of “uncertainty.” This prolonged anxiety is eroding American citizens' cognition and judgment.
6 Opportunity Cost Innovation and possibilities never realized due to institutional constraints Strategic mono-culture: The U.S. has only two tools for Iran — “military strikes” and “economic sanctions.” Other possibilities — such as regional security cooperation, energy agreements, diplomatic breakthroughs — have never been seriously considered due to institutional and ideological constraints. Opportunity cost here manifests as: the U.S. has never attempted to construct a Middle East order not dependent on war — and this “institutional loss of imagination” is one of the roots of its hegemonic decline.
7 Transfer Pathways Mechanisms that shift value from public to private domains War privatization: The core of the U.S. war machine is the “military-industrial complex” — public expenditures on war are transformed into private corporate profits through defense contracts. Boeing, Raytheon, Lockheed Martin, and others secured massive orders from the war, while public debt continued to accumulate. The transfer pathway is clear: war costs are borne by the entire population, while war profits are enjoyed by a few capitalists — precisely the core mechanism by which governance legitimacy is hollowed out.
8 Closure Degree The system's ability to self-reinforce and exclude external challenges Information cocoon: The U.S. media—think tank—Congress—military-industrial complex forms a tightly closed loop. Voices criticizing the war are labeled “unpatriotic,” while pro-war narratives are amplified without limit. This closed loop excludes not only external challenges (such as international anti-war sentiment) but also internal reflection (such as questioning the war's legitimacy). The higher the closure degree, the harder it is for the U.S. to withdraw from erroneous strategies — because admitting error costs far more than continuing the error.

III. Comprehensive Diagnosis: The Legitimacy Collapse of Hegemony

From the eight diagnoses above, a complete causal chain emerges:

  • Information Asymmetry Cost keeps both U.S. decision-makers and the public in a “cognitive bubble”;
  • Waiting Cost and Repetition Cost continuously reduce war efficiency;
  • Collapse Cost exposes systemic vulnerability in logistics and supply chains;
  • Psychological Cost and Opportunity Cost gradually erode strategic imagination;
  • Transfer Pathways privatize war profits, further corroding public trust;
  • Closure Degree prevents the system from self-correcting, falling into a vicious cycle of “persevering in error.”

This is what the GL Framework defines as “governance legitimacy failure” — not a single policy error, but a systemic collapse of the entire governance structure.

⚔️ Conclusion
The Iran war is not an “external shock” to U.S. hegemony, but the “externalized manifestation” of its internal legitimacy crisis. America's predicament lies not in being unable to defeat Iran, but in having lost the governance legitimacy to answer “why we fight” — whether to its domestic public, its allies, or the international community. When a hegemony's “governance output” consists only of war and chaos, its collapse is no longer a question of “if,” but “when.”
