Harvard Doesn't Select Geniuses, It Selects Heirs:
How AI Is Finally Tearing Off the Ivy League Packaging

Why the vast majority of elite university graduates are mediocre — and why the system is about to collapse

A structural truth about elite universities has become increasingly difficult to ignore: the vast majority of students at Harvard, Yale, Cambridge, and Oxford are not brilliant — they are mediocre. Only a tiny fraction are truly exceptional. The rest are products of a packaging system.

This is not sour grapes. This is a structural observation backed by decades of data.

I. The Data from Boston: Who Actually Gets In?

If elite universities truly selected the best minds, their student bodies would reflect the full range of talent. They do not. They reflect the full range of wealth.

Harvard University:

  • Over two-thirds of Harvard students come from the top 20% of U.S. income earners.
  • More than half come from the top 10%.
  • Nearly one in seven comes from the top 1% — families earning over $630,000 per year.
  • For 100 years, the proportion of students from the bottom 20% has remained at just 5% — virtually unchanged.

Boston College: Median family income: $194,000. Only 1.6% from low-income backgrounds.

Boston University: Median family income: $141,000.

Legacy Admissions:

  • Legacy applicants are admitted at rates 2 to 5 times higher than non-legacy applicants.
  • Harvard's legacy admission rate is 33.6% vs. the overall rate of 5.9% — a 5.7x advantage.
  • Even with identical SAT scores, students from the wealthiest families are nearly 5 times more likely to be admitted than middle-class students.

📊 The Numbers Don't Lie

This is not a meritocracy. It is a system of class reproduction disguised as selection.

If you are selecting the best, why do 2/3 of students come from the top 20%? Why has the bottom 20% been stuck at 5% for a century?

II. The Tiny Fraction of True Geniuses: They Are the "Showpieces"

It is true that every generation produces a handful of truly brilliant people from these institutions — individuals who actually change the world. But here is the structural trick: the system uses these rare exceptions as "showpieces."

  • They prove the system can select geniuses — so the system looks legitimate.
  • They provide cover for the other 90% of graduates who are merely mediocre.
  • They allow the institution to say: "Look at our alumni — Einstein, Zuckerberg, Musk."

The exceptional few provide legitimacy for the mediocre many. Without them, the packaging would be visible. With them, the system appears to work — even though it is mostly reproducing privilege, not talent.

🎭 The Showpiece Function

The system needs a few geniuses to justify the existence of the mediocre majority.

Without the showpieces, the packaging would collapse. With them, the packaging appears real.

III. Why "Mediocre" Is Actually a Feature, Not a Bug

If the ruling class is so mediocre, why hasn't the system collapsed?

The answer is: Mediocrity is more stable than excellence.

  • Excellence demands change. An excellent leader would question the system, restructure it, and disrupt the privileges of the elite. That is a threat.
  • Mediocrity demands preservation. A mediocre leader will follow procedure, protect the status quo, and ensure the system reproduces itself. That is exactly what the elite wants.

The system does not select for the best. It selects for the safest. It selects for people who will not rock the boat. It selects for people who will protect the existing order.

Mediocrity is not a failure of the system—it is the system's intended output.

Together, they form a closed loop that packages mediocrity as excellence.

VII. The Atlantic Council Incident: When the Packaging Tore

In February 2024, the Atlantic Council — a Washington-based think tank funded by the U.S. government and NATO — hosted a panel discussion on "China and the Global South." On stage sat a U.S. Assistant Secretary of State. He had all the packaging: elite degrees, decades of diplomatic credentials, a Washington title.

His argument was simple: China's influence in the developing world was expanding, but these countries "did not fully understand the risks of cooperating with China."

The subtext was clear: You chose China because you are ignorant.

In the audience sat Bilahari Kausikan, a former Singaporean diplomat. He had no Ivy League degree, no Washington title. But his résumé had something else: decades of hands-on experience working with the Global South.

He took the microphone and said: "I think China understands the needs of the Global South better than the West does."

He continued: China does not lecture these countries. It does not interfere in their internal affairs. It does not block their development in the name of "human rights." China talks to them about ports, railways, power plants, and hospitals.

The reaction was not applause. It was silence, followed by scattered laughter — the kind that says: "He just said what we were all thinking."

The Assistant Secretary did not respond. He could not. He had never actually worked with the Global South. All his "expertise" came from Washington offices and think-tank reports. His packaging — the degrees, the title, the credentials — fell apart in front of someone with real experience.

This is what happens when the packaging tears. An American official, born into elite institutions, tried to explain why the world wasn't listening to America — by calling the world ignorant. And he was exposed by a diplomat without any of his credentials, but with something far more valuable: real knowledge.

The laughter was not cruelty. It was the sound of packaging collapsing. The sound of a lifetime of institutional privilege being shattered in five seconds by a single fact.

VIII. The China Comparison: A Different Logic

If the Anglo-Saxon elite university is a "machine of class reproduction," then China's elite universities have, at least in some measure, preserved the function of a "channel for social mobility."

The key difference: China has no legacy admissions.

In the United States, legacy applicants are admitted at rates 2 to 5 times higher than non-legacy applicants. Harvard's legacy admission rate is 33.6%, while its overall rate is just 5.9%. In China, the core criterion for university admission is — and has always been — the Gaokao (national college entrance exam) score.

This is not to say that China's elite universities are free of class disparities. A 2025 study published in the journal Economic Development and Cultural Change found that in China, children from the top 20% of income families are 2.3 times more likely to enter elite universities than those from the bottom 20%.

2.3 times.

In the United States, that same figure is 11.2 times.

In other words, class disparities exist in China — but their severity is less than one-fifth of what they are in the United States.

The study also found a crucial detail: the Gaokao score thresholds for elite university admission are "income-neutral." That is, once you meet the score requirement, your family background does not determine whether you are admitted. In the United States, even with identical SAT scores, students from wealthy families are nearly 5 times more likely to be admitted than middle-class students. In China, the same score does not produce different outcomes based on who your parents are.

These are two fundamentally different institutional logics:

United States China
Admissions basis Test scores + legacy + donor status + athletic recruitment Gaokao score (primarily)
Legacy advantage 2–5x admission rate None
Top 20% vs. bottom 20% admission gap 11.2x 2.3x
Systemic function Class reproduction Social mobility (limited but real)

China's Gaokao system is far from perfect. It imposes immense pressure, reflects deep urban-rural educational inequalities, and carries the brutal weight of "one exam determines a lifetime." But at the level of institutional design, it preserves a channel that is "based on score, not on birth." A child from a pastoral region in Qinghai and a child from Beijing's Haidian district face the same exam paper in the examination hall.

Since 1949, it has been the nationwide unified Gaokao system — together with universal basic education — that has ensured countless working-class and peasant families' children a path to upward mobility. That path is narrowing, but it still exists. In the Anglo-Saxon world, that path was never designed for them in the first place.

IX. AI: The Packaging Ripper

The elite university's packaging has survived for centuries because it controlled the means of certification. But AI is making certification irrelevant.

Brown University's brutal experiment: A professor gave students a take-home midterm. The class average jumped from 65-80 to 96, with 40 students scoring 100%. He suspected AI cheating, so he gave an in-person final exam. 18 students dropped the course, 9 didn't show up, and those who took it averaged 48.6 — with 19 failing.

The experiment tore open the truth: how much of "elite excellence" is just packaging?

Stanford CS graduates can't find jobs: Since ChatGPT's arrival, employment for junior developers (ages 22-25) has dropped nearly 20%. Vectara's CEO: "We don't need junior developers anymore. AI now writes code that surpasses many fresh graduates from elite universities." Anthropic's CEO: 70% to 90% of the company's code is now written by Claude.

LinkedIn's CEO: The era of employers prioritizing degrees is rapidly ending. 86% of financial services executives say AI skills training is more valuable than an MBA. 91% are increasing pay for employees with AI capabilities.

Even Palantir's CEO (who holds a philosophy PhD) said: "If you went to an elite school and studied philosophy, I hope you have something else, because that degree is hard to sell." He believes only two groups will survive: those with vocational training, and neurodivergent people. The "generalists" from elite schools — those packaged mediocrities — are being eliminated.

🤖 How AI Unpacks the System

  • Signal scarcity (degrees are hard to fake) → AI makes knowledge "ask and obtain," anyone can perform like a trained professional
  • Knowledge monopoly (only elite schools can teach specialized knowledge) → AI provides personalized tutoring at near-zero marginal cost
  • Alumni networks (closed class loops) → employers no longer need alumni referrals; GitHub profiles are more persuasive than transcripts
  • The meritocracy myth → when AI enables everyone to do what elite graduates can do, the myth collapses

X. The New Standard: Delivery Over Diplomas

Fei-Fei Li put it clearly: "When we interview software engineers, I personally think their degree is no longer that important. What matters now is what you have learned, what tools you have used, and how quickly you can upskill with these tools."

  • GitHub portfolios count more than transcripts

In the AI era, the only way to prove your ability is to produce real work.

八、中國對比：另一種邏輯

如果說盎格魯-薩克遜的精英大學是「階級再生產的機器」，那麼中國的精英大學在某種程度上，仍然保留了「社會流動的通道」的功能。

關鍵差異在於：中國沒有「傳承錄取」。

在美國，校友子女的錄取率是非校友子女的2到5倍；哈佛的傳承生錄取率高達33.6%，而整體錄取率僅5.9%。在中國，大學錄取的核心標準只有一個——高考分數。

這不是說中國的精英大學完全沒有階級差異。一項2025年發表於《經濟發展與文化變遷》期刊的研究發現，在中國，收入前20%家庭的孩子進入精英大學的機率，是底層20%家庭孩子的2.3倍。

2.3倍。

而在美國，這個數字是11.2倍。

換句話說，中國的階級差距確實存在，但它的嚴重程度——不到美國的五分之一。

這項研究還有一個關鍵發現：精英大學錄取的分數線規則是「收入中立的」。也就是說，一旦你達到分數線，你的家庭背景不會決定你是否被錄取。在美國，即使SAT成績相同，富豪家庭學生的錄取率也比中產家庭高出近5倍。在中國，同樣的分數，不會因為你的父母是誰而有不同的結果。

制度功能 階級再生產 社會流動（有限但真實）

中國的高考制度並不完美。它有著巨大的壓力、城鄉教育資源的不均衡、以及「一試定終身」的殘酷。但它至少在制度設計上，保留了一條「不看出身、只看分數」的通道。一個青海牧區的孩子和一個北京海淀的孩子，在考場上面對的是同一張試卷。

1949年以來，正是全國統一的高考制度和覆蓋全國的基礎教育，保障了無數工农子弟獲得向上流動的機會。這條通道越來越窄，但它仍然存在。而在盎格魯-薩克遜世界，那條通道從一開始就是為有錢人保留的。

📊 來源： 賈瑞雪、李宏彬、孟嶺生，〈中國的精英大學教育與社會流動〉，《經濟發展與文化變遷》第73卷第3期（2025年4月），第1145-1186頁。

九、AI：撕掉包裝的機器

頂尖大學的包裝系統能存活幾個世紀，是因為它控制了認證手段。但AI正在讓認證變得無關緊要。

布朗大學的殘酷實驗：教授讓學生把期中考帶回家作答。全班平均分從65-80飆升到96分，40名學生拿滿分。他懷疑學生用AI作弊，於是在期末舉行現場考試——18人退課，9人缺考，實際考試的人平均分48.6，19人不及格。

這場實驗撕開了一個真相：名校的「優秀」有多少是包裝出來的？

史丹佛CS畢業生也找不到工作：自ChatGPT問世以來，22至25歲初級軟體開發人員的就業率下降了近20%。Vectara CEO：「我們已經不需要初級開發者了。AI寫程式碼的水平，已經超過了很多名校畢業的應屆生。」Anthropic CEO透露，公司70%到90%的程式碼由Claude編寫。

LinkedIn CEO：雇主優先考慮大學學位的時代正在迅速結束。86%的金融服務高管認為AI技能培訓比MBA更有價值。91%正在提高具備AI能力員工的薪酬。

甚至連Palantir CEO——本人擁有哲學博士學位——也直言：「如果你去了名校讀哲學，希望你還有點別的本事，因為那個學位很難推銷出去。」他認為AI時代只有兩類人能生存：接受過職業技能培訓的人，和神經多樣性人群。名校的「通才」——那些靠包裝上位的庸才——正被淘汰。

🤖 AI如何拆解名校包裝

  • 信號稀缺（文憑難以偽造）→ AI讓知識「可說即得」，任何人都能表現得像受過訓練
  • 知識壟斷（只有名校能傳授專業知識）→ AI工具以近乎零邊際成本提供個人化輔導
  • 校友網絡（階級封閉循環）→ 雇主不再需要靠校友推薦來篩選人才，GitHub檔案比大學成績單更有說服力
  • 菁英主義神話 → 當AI讓所有人都能完成名校畢業生的工作時，神話自然瓦解

十、新標準：實戰力取代文憑

李飛飛說得很清楚：「當我們面試軟體工程師時，我個人認為他們的學位對我們來說已經不那麼重要了。現在更重要的是你學到了什麼、你使用了哪些工具，以及你能多快地提升自己使用這些工具的能力。」

矽谷的用人標準已經改變：

  • 適應能力和AI流利度比「高級學位」更重要
  • GitHub個人檔案比大學成績單更有說服力
  • 具體的商業價值比學士學位更受歡迎

在AI時代，證明自己能力的唯一方式，就是拿出真實的作品。

十一、結論：包裝終於正在撕裂

證據已經非常明確：頂尖大學的絕大多數學生都是平庸的。少數例外是真實存在的，但他們是「樣品」——用來為一個主要再生產特權的系統提供合法性。

平庸可以被包裝、被認證、被偽裝。但不可能永遠隱藏。AI正在一層層撕掉包裝紙。大西洋理事會的笑聲是一個警告。史丹佛CS畢業生找不到工作是一個警告。布朗大學的實驗是一個警告。

世界不再對標籤印象深刻。世界不再等待。

哈佛不能讓一個平庸的人變得卓越。它只能讓他們看起來卓越——直到AI撕掉那層包裝紙。

💡 最終真相

平庸不是系統的缺陷。它是系統最偉大的成就。

頂尖大學是將平庸包裝成卓越、將認證包裝成資格、將繼承包裝成功績的機器。

但包裝正在撕裂。AI正在撕碎它。而當它完全撕裂時，平庸的統治者將沒有什麼可以躲藏。

他們將被揭露為他們一直以來的樣子：無能、不安、無關緊要。
