Algorithm and Governance:
How AI Ends Procedural Justice

算法与治理——AI如何终结程序正义
August 2026 · 2026年8月

I. The Algorithm Arrives

In 2026, a quiet revolution occurred that most governance theorists missed.

China's revised Government Procurement Law added a new chapter: “Digital Procurement.” For the first time in any major legal system, algorithms were given a formal role in government decision‑making.

This is not a minor technical update. It is a fundamental challenge to the entire framework of procedural legitimacy.

When algorithms begin to assist — and eventually replace — human decision‑making, the old questions of procedural justice become obsolete. The new question is: “Does the algorithm deliver results?”

II. The Old Logic: Procedure as Justification

For centuries, governance legitimacy rested on procedure:

  • Elections were held
  • Laws were passed
  • Hearings were conducted
  • Votes were counted

If these procedures were followed, the outcome was legitimate — regardless of its quality. The process was the justification.

This logic worked when human judgment was the only available tool. But human judgment is being replaced.

III. The New Logic: Algorithm as Decision‑Maker

Algorithms are fundamentally different from human procedures:

  • They are predictive, not deliberative. They do not debate. They calculate.
  • They are optimized for outcomes, not processes. They are trained to maximize results, not to follow steps.
  • They are continuous, not periodic. They do not wait for elections. They operate in real time.

When an algorithm allocates resources, evaluates bids, or predicts demand, it is not “following a procedure” in the traditional sense. It is producing an outcome.

And if the outcome is good, who cares about the procedure?

IV. The 2026 Turning Point: Digital Procurement

China's 2026 Procurement Law reform is the first major legal framework to formally integrate algorithms into governance:

  • Algorithmic bid evaluation — AI systems assess vendor proposals based on performance data, not just compliance
  • Real‑time price monitoring — algorithms track market prices and adjust procurement strategies dynamically
  • Outcome‑based verification — algorithms measure actual delivery against contract terms

This is not “digitalization” in the Western sense — adding computers to existing processes. This is process replacement. The algorithm does not assist the human. The algorithm subsumes the human.

V. Why Algorithmic Governance Challenges Procedural Legitimacy

Procedural legitimacy relies on transparency, participation, and accountability. Algorithms challenge all three:

  • Transparency — algorithms are black boxes. Even their creators cannot fully explain their decisions.
  • Participation — algorithms do not hold hearings or accept public comments. They process data.
  • Accountability — when an algorithm makes a mistake, who is responsible? The programmer? The user? The machine?

The old framework has no answer to these questions. Procedural legitimacy is not equipped for the algorithmic age.

VI. The New Source of Legitimacy: Algorithmic Performance

If algorithms cannot provide procedural legitimacy, where does their legitimacy come from?

The answer is: performance.

  • Does the algorithm reduce costs?
  • Does the algorithm improve delivery?
  • Does the algorithm produce better outcomes than humans?

If the answer is yes, the algorithm is legitimate — regardless of its opacity.

This is not a hypothetical. It is already happening.

VII. The GFI Framework: Algorithmic Legitimacy

Under the GFI Governance Legitimacy Framework, a new category must be added:

Source of LegitimacyQuestionExample
Procedural“Was it done correctly?”Elections, hearings, votes
Performance“Was it done at all?”Infrastructure, poverty reduction
Algorithmic“Did the algorithm produce the best outcome?”AI procurement, predictive allocation

The algorithm does not care about procedure. It only cares about results.

This is why algorithmic governance is so threatening to the Western elite. It bypasses the entire structure of procedural legitimacy that their power depends on.

VIII. The Western Dilemma

Western governments face an impossible choice:

  • Adopt algorithms — and abandon procedural legitimacy
  • Reject algorithms — and fall further behind in governance performance

Either way, the old framework loses.

This is why the West is both terrified of Chinese AI and unable to compete with it. It is not a technology gap. It is a legitimacy gap.

IX. The Future: Governance Without Procedure

What happens when algorithms become the primary decision‑makers in governance?

  • Elections will become less important — algorithms do not need to be elected
  • Debate will become less relevant — algorithms do not deliberate
  • Procedure will become obsolete — algorithms only optimize for outcomes

This is not a dystopia. This is the logical conclusion of the performance‑based governance model.

When citizens demand results, and algorithms deliver them, the question “how was this decision made?” becomes irrelevant. Only one question matters: “Did it work?”

X. Conclusion: The Algorithmic Era

The era of procedural justice is ending. The era of algorithmic justice is beginning.

This is not a prediction. It is already happening in China's procurement system, in predictive policing, in resource allocation, in public health management.

The West can continue to defend procedural legitimacy. But the algorithm does not care about procedures.

The algorithm cares about results. And so do citizens.

This is the message of 2026. This is the message of GFI. This is the message that the Western elite refuses to hear.

“Procedural justice is a human invention. The algorithm was not consulted.”

一、算法的到来

2026年，一场被大多数治理理论家忽视的静默革命发生了。

中国修订后的《政府采购法》增加了一个新章节：“政府采购数字化专章”。算法首次在任何主要法律体系中获得了在政府决策中的正式角色。

这不是一次微小的技术更新。这是 对整个程序正当性框架的根本性挑战。

当算法开始辅助——并最终取代——人类决策时，程序正义的老问题就变得过时了。新的问题是：“算法是否交付了结果？”

二、旧逻辑：程序作为理由

几个世纪以来，治理正当性建立在程序之上：

  • 举行了选举
  • 通过了法律
  • 召开了听证会
  • 计算了选票

如果这些程序被遵循，结果就是正当的——无论其质量如何。程序本身就是理由。

当人类判断是唯一可用工具时，这个逻辑是有效的。但人类判断正在被取代。

三、新逻辑：算法作为决策者

算法在根本上不同于人类程序：

  • 它们是预测性的，而非审议性的。它们不辩论。它们计算。
  • 它们为结果而非过程而优化。它们被训练来最大化结果，而不是遵循步骤。
  • 它们是连续的，而非周期性的。它们不等选举。它们实时运行。

当一个算法分配资源、评估投标或预测需求时，它不是传统意义上的“遵循程序”。它是 产生一个结果。

如果结果是好的，谁在乎程序？

四、2026年转折点：数字化采购

中国2026年《政府采购法》修订是第一个将算法正式整合到治理中的主要法律框架：

  • 算法化评标——AI系统基于绩效数据而非仅合规性评估供应商提案
  • 实时价格监控——算法跟踪市场价格并动态调整采购策略
  • 基于成果的验证——算法根据合同条款测量实际交付

这不是西方意义上的“数字化”——将计算机添加到现有流程中。这是 流程替代。 算法不是辅助人类。算法 取代了人类。

五、为什么算法治理挑战程序正当性

程序正当性依赖于透明度、参与和问责。算法挑战了所有三者：

  • 透明度——算法是黑箱。即使是它们的创造者也无法完全解释它们的决策。
  • 参与——算法不举行听证会或不接受公众意见。它们处理数据。
  • 问责——当算法犯错时，谁负责？程序员？用户？机器？

旧框架无法回答这些问题。程序正当性不具备应对算法时代的能力。

六、正当性的新来源：算法绩效

如果算法不能提供程序正当性，它们的正当性来自哪里？

答案是：绩效。

  • 算法是否降低了成本？
  • 算法是否改善了交付？
  • 算法是否产生了比人类更好的结果？

如果答案是肯定的，算法就是正当的——无论其不透明性如何。

这不是假设。它正在发生。

正当性来源问题示例

绩效“它是否被执行了？”基础设施、减贫

这就是为什么算法治理对西方精英如此具有威胁性。它绕过了他们权力所依赖的整个程序正当性结构。

八、西方的困境

西方政府面临一个不可能的选择：

  • 采用算法——然后放弃程序正当性
  • 拒绝算法——然后在治理绩效上进一步落后

无论哪种方式，旧框架都输了。

这就是为什么西方既恐惧中国AI又无法与之竞争的原因。这不是技术差距。这是正当性差距。

当算法成为治理中的主要决策者时会发生什么？

  • 选举将变得不那么重要——算法不需要被选举
  • 辩论将变得不那么相关——算法不审议
  • 程序将变得过时——算法只为结果优化

这不是反乌托邦。这是 基于绩效的治理模型的逻辑结论。

当公民要求结果，而算法交付结果时，“这个决定是如何做出的？”这个问题变得无关紧要。只有一个问题重要：“它有效吗？”
